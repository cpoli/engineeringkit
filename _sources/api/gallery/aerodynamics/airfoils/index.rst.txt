Satisfying flow tangency gives a lift slope of exactly 2\pi per radian for every thin section, while camber only shifts the curve by a zero-lift angle given by an integral of the camber slope. naca4_zero_lift_angle evaluates that integral for NACA four-digit camber lines, and thin_airfoil_cl gives the lift line.">

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="At the Douglas Aircraft Company, John Hess and A. M. O. Smith replaced a body by straight surface panels, each with its own uniform source strength, and solved for the strengths that make the surface a streamline. Their 1967 review in Progress in Aerospace Sciences added a single vortex strength shared by every panel. With the Kutta condition at the trailing edge, that let the method carry lift. panel_method implements it. This example checks it against the exact flow round a circular cylinder, then shows what it adds to thin-airfoil theory: the leading-edge suction peak and the extra lift slope of a thick section.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Munk&#x27;s and Glauert&#x27;s thin-airfoil theory predicts more than lift. The pitching moment about the quarter chord, c_{m,c/4} = \tfrac{\pi}{4}(A_2 - A_1) (~engineeringkit.aerodynamics.naca4_moment_coefficient), depends only on the camber line and not on the angle of attack. The quarter chord is therefore the aerodynamic centre. A measured section&#x27;s centre sits at x_{ac}/c = \tfrac14 - m_0/a_0 (~engineeringkit.aerodynamics.aerodynamic_center), where m_0 is its residual moment slope. This example reproduces Anderson&#x27;s c_{m,c/4} = -0.053 for the NACA 2412 and finds the aerodynamic centre from panel-method moments.">
