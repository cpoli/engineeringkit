    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Daniel Bernoulli&#x27;s Hydrodynamica (1738) traded pressure for speed along a streamline: p + \tfrac12\rho V^2 is constant. A pitot tube brings the flow to rest and reads the total pressure; the static port reads p; their difference is the dynamic pressure q = \tfrac12\rho V^2 from dynamic_pressure. Inverting it gives the airspeed, but only if the right density is used: the same pitot reading means a higher true airspeed at altitude.">

Dividing a measured force by qS removes the dependence on speed and size, so tests at one speed predict forces at another. This example reduces synthetic whirling-arm measurements with lift_coefficient and drag_coefficient and reads the best glide ratio off the polar with glide_ratio.">

After their 1901 glider lifted far less than predicted, Wilbur and Orville Wright measured k \approx 0.0033 in their own wind tunnel; the modern value, \tfrac12\rho at sea level, is 0.00256. Since the minimum flying speed V_s = \sqrt{2W/(\rho S C_{L,\max})} (~engineeringkit.aerodynamics.stall_speed) scales as 1/\sqrt{k}, Smeaton&#x27;s value under-predicts the take-off speed of the 1903 Flyer by about 30%.">
