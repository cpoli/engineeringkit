Examples
========

.. include:: /_generated/nav/aerodynamics.rst

This gallery walks through every public feature of ``engineeringkit.aerodynamics``,
one breakthrough per script: dynamic pressure and force coefficients,
circulation lift, airfoil sections, finite wings, cruise performance,
boundary layers, compressible flow and the standard atmosphere.

:doc:`Units, assumptions and numerical validation </guides/aerodynamics>`
gives the physical validity limits. Each script is self-contained and can be
run directly with ``python examples/aerodynamics/<section>/<script>.py``.

Sections
--------

- **forces** -- Bernoulli's dynamic pressure, Cayley's lift/drag split,
  Lilienthal's coefficients and the Wrights' correction of Smeaton's coefficient.
- **circulation** -- the Kutta-Joukowski theorem on a spinning cylinder.
- **airfoils** -- NACA four-digit sections and thin-airfoil theory.
- **wings** -- Prandtl's lifting line, induced drag and the Oswald drag polar.
- **performance** -- the Breguet range equation.
- **boundary_layer** -- Reynolds similarity, the Blasius layer and turbulent skin friction.
- **compressible** -- sound speed, the Mach cone, stagnation states,
  Prandtl-Glauert, normal shocks and the de Laval nozzle.
- **atmosphere** -- the International Standard Atmosphere.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Martin Kutta (1902) and Nikolai Joukowski (1906) showed that the lift per unit span on any two-dimensional body in a steady inviscid stream is L&#x27; = \rho V \Gamma, where \Gamma is the circulation around the body (~engineeringkit.aerodynamics.kutta_joukowski_lift).     <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Newton&#x27;s Principia (1687) predicted the speed of sound assuming the compressions are isothermal, a = \sqrt{p/\rho} = \sqrt{RT}, about 15% below measurement. Pierre-Simon Laplace (1816) argued that the compressions are too fast to exchange heat, so they are adiabatic and the stiffness rises by \gamma: a = \sqrt{\gamma R T} (~engineeringkit.aerodynamics.speed_of_sound).     <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Daniel Bernoulli&#x27;s Hydrodynamica (1738) traded pressure for speed along a streamline: p + \tfrac12\rho V^2 is constant. A pitot tube brings the flow to rest and reads the total pressure; the static port reads p; their difference is the dynamic pressure q = \tfrac12\rho V^2 from dynamic_pressure.
