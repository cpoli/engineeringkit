Performance
-----------

Jet and propeller cruise range from aerodynamic efficiency and engine fuel consumption.

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Louis Breguet&#x27;s range formula (1923) follows from three facts: thrust equals drag, drag equals weight divided by L/D, and fuel flow is the thrust-specific fuel consumption times thrust. Integrating the weight loss gives R = \frac{V}{g c_T}\frac{L}{D}\ln\frac{W_i}{W_f} (~engineeringkit.aerodynamics.breguet_range): range is the product of engine efficiency, aerodynamic efficiency and a logarithmic fuel fraction. This example checks it against a time-stepped cruise and shows why airliners fly near the best-glide lift coefficient.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Breguet&#x27;s range equation was first written for propeller aircraft, the only kind there was. Shaft power P = DV/\eta_{pr} burns fuel at a rate set by the engine&#x27;s power-specific consumption c_P. Drag is W/(L/D), so the speed cancels and R = \frac{\eta_{pr}}{g c_P}\frac{L}{D}\ln\frac{W_i}{W_f} (~engineeringkit.aerodynamics.breguet_range_propeller). A propeller aircraft goes furthest at the speed of maximum L/D, which is slower than a jet&#x27;s best-range speed. This example checks the formula against a time-stepped cruise and plots range against speed for a light aircraft.">
