Finite wings
------------

Lifting-line theory and Glauert's Fourier solution, induced drag and the parabolic drag polar.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Ludwig Prandtl&#x27;s Tragflügeltheorie (1918) modelled a wing as a bound vortex of varying strength shedding a sheet of trailing vortices. The downwash they induce lowers the effective angle of attack, so a finite wing&#x27;s lift slope is a = a_0/(1 + a_0/(\pi e AR)) (~engineeringkit.aerodynamics.finite_wing_lift_slope). This example solves Glauert&#x27;s Fourier form of the lifting-line equation for a rectangular wing and compares the computed slope with the closed form.">

This example builds spanwise loads, computes their induced drag from the Fourier coefficients, and confirms the ellipse wins.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="W. Bailey Oswald&#x27;s NACA Report 408 (1933) folded every source of lift-dependent drag of a whole aeroplane into one efficiency factor, so its drag polar becomes C_D = C_{D0} + C_L^2/(\pi e AR) (~engineeringkit.aerodynamics.drag_polar). The maximum lift-to-drag ratio lies where a line from the origin is tangent to the polar, at C_L = \sqrt{\pi e AR C_{D0}} (~engineeringkit.aerodynamics.best_glide_cl). The empirical oswald_efficiency_estimate supplies e for straight wings.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Prandtl&#x27;s lifting-line equation is an integro-differential equation for the spanwise circulation. In The Elements of Aerofoil and Airscrew Theory (1926) Hermann Glauert solved it for any planform. He wrote the circulation as a Fourier sine series in \theta, with y = -\tfrac b2\cos\theta, and enforced the equation at as many stations as there are terms (~engineeringkit.aerodynamics.lifting_line). The first coefficient fixes the lift and the others add induced drag, so the span efficiency is e = 1/(1 + \delta) with \delta = \sum_{n&gt;1} nA_n^2/A_1^2. This example recovers the elliptic optimum exactly, then sweeps the taper ratio of straight wings to reproduce Glauert&#x27;s induced-drag factor \delta.">
