Detection
---------

The matched filter, and error rates of coherent BPSK, QPSK, M-PSK and M-QAM in Gaussian noise.

For two antipodal signals the answer is a correlator (or matched filter) followed by a sign decision, with bit-error rate Q(\sqrt{2E_b/N_0}) (~engineeringkit.communications.ber_bpsk_awgn, q_function). A Monte Carlo simulation of bpsk through noise reproduces the curve.">

Cosine and sine carriers at the same frequency are orthogonal, so each can carry an independent BPSK stream. Gray-labelled QPSK (~engineeringkit.communications.qpsk) sends two bits per symbol in the bandwidth of one. Its bit-error rate per unit E_b/N_0 equals that of BPSK (~engineeringkit.communications.ber_qpsk_awgn).">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Working on radar at RCA Laboratories, Dwight North asked which receiving filter makes a known pulse stand out best above white noise. His wartime report answered it: the filter whose impulse response is the pulse reversed in time (and conjugated), matched_filter. Sampled at the end of the pulse, its output signal-to-noise ratio is 2E/N_0 (~engineeringkit.communications.matched_filter_snr). That depends only on the pulse&#x27;s energy, not its shape, and no linear filter does better: the bound is the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Charles Cahn asked what happens when a carrier&#x27;s phase takes not two but M values, so that each symbol carries \log_2 M bits. The points sit on a circle (~engineeringkit.communications.psk_constellation) and crowd together as M grows: the distance between neighbours is 2\sin(\pi/M). Every doubling of M beyond four costs about 6 dB of E_s/N_0. Cahn concluded that multiphase signalling trades bandwidth for signal-to-noise ratio more efficiently than multilevel amplitude signalling. The exact symbol-error rate of minimum-distance detection (~engineeringkit.communications.minimum_distance_detect) is given by Craig&#x27;s integral (~engineeringkit.communications.ser_mpsk_awgn).">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Phase-shift keying wastes the inside of the circle. C. N. Campopiano and B. G. Glazer modulated the amplitudes of two carriers in quadrature independently, so the points fill a square grid (~engineeringkit.communications.qam_constellation). Each rail is a \sqrt M-level amplitude decision, and the exact symbol-error rate follows from one rail&#x27;s error probability (~engineeringkit.communications.ser_mqam_awgn). At 16 points and above, square QAM needs several decibels less power than M-PSK, which is why every modem from the 1970s to Wi-Fi and 5G uses it.">
