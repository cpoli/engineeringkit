Examples
========

.. include:: /_generated/nav/communications.rst

This gallery walks through every public feature of
``engineeringkit.communications``, one breakthrough per script: decibels,
radio propagation and link budgets, antennas, thermal noise, analog
modulation and its noise performance, information, pulse shaping and
capacity, optimum detection, digitization, source and channel codes,
fading and diversity, and OFDM.

:doc:`Units, assumptions and numerical validation </guides/communications>`
gives the physical validity limits. Each script is self-contained and can be
run directly with ``python examples/communications/<section>/<script>.py``.

Sections
--------

- **decibels** -- the Bell System transmission unit.
- **propagation** -- Hertz's waves, the Friis transmission formula and the Telstar link budget.
- **antennas** -- Friis's effective aperture and Carter's half-wave dipole.
- **noise** -- Johnson-Nyquist noise and Friis's cascaded noise factor.
- **modulation** -- Fessenden's AM, Carson's suppressed carrier, Armstrong's FM and Crosby's noise analysis of FM against AM.
- **information** -- Nyquist's signalling rate and raised-cosine pulses, Hartley's law, Shannon's entropy and capacity, and Huffman's code.
- **detection** -- Kotel'nikov's optimum BPSK receiver, quadrature (QPSK) modulation, North's matched filter, Cahn's M-PSK and square QAM.
- **digitization** -- Reeves's pulse-code modulation and delta modulation.
- **coding** -- the Hamming(7,4) code, Elias's convolutional codes, the cyclic redundancy check and Viterbi decoding.
- **fading** -- Rayleigh's random phasors and diversity combining.
- **multiplexing** -- DFT-based OFDM with a cyclic prefix.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Friis first wrote his transmission formula not with gains but with the antennas&#x27; effective areas: P_r/P_t = A_t A_r/(d\lambda)^2.     <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Frustrated by weekend computer runs aborted by a single bad relay, Richard Hamming (1950) designed codes that correct errors rather than merely detect them. In the (7,4) code three parity bits sit at positions 1, 2 and 4. When one bit flips, the failing parity checks, read as a binary number, give its position (~engineeringkit.communications.hamming74_encode, hamming74_decode). Elias proved that such codes are at least as good as block codes.     <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Alec Reeves, an engineer at International Telephone and Telegraph&#x27;s Paris laboratory, proposed sampling a speech waveform, rounding each sample to one of 2^N levels and sending the level number as N on-off pulses (~engineeringkit.communications.pcm_encode, pcm_decode). Rounding adds a small, fixed amount of noise, about 6.02N + 1.76 dB below a full-scale sine (~engineeringkit.communications.pcm_sqnr_db). In exchange, a repeater has only to decide whether each pulse is present, so noise does not accumulate along the line. The vacuum tubes of 1938 made PCM impractical. It waited for the transistor and the Bell System&#x27;s T1 carrier of 1962, which used companding (~engineeringkit.communications.mu_law_compress) to keep quiet speech above the quantization noise.">

Rayleigh fading from many scattered paths, and diversity combining.


.. raw:: html

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Lord Rayleigh asked what amplitude results when a large number of vibrations of the same pitch but arbitrary phase are added, a question from optics and acoustics. The answer is a density f(r) = (2r/\Omega)e^{-r^2/\Omega}, now called the Rayleigh distribution (~engineeringkit.communications.rayleigh_pdf). Seventy years later it described the mobile radio channel: a signal reaching a car by many scattered paths fades deeply and often. Averaged over the fades, the bit-error rate of BPSK falls only as 1/4\bar\gamma (~engineeringkit.communications.ber_bpsk_rayleigh) instead of exponentially, so a fading link needs tens of decibels more power than a steady one.">
