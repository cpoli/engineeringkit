Examples
========

.. include:: /_generated/nav/communications.rst

This gallery walks through every public feature of
``engineeringkit.communications``, one breakthrough per script: decibels,
radio propagation and link budgets, thermal noise, analog modulation,
information and capacity, optimum detection, error-correcting codes and OFDM.

:doc:`Units, assumptions and numerical validation </guides/communications>`
gives the physical validity limits. Each script is self-contained and can be
run directly with ``python examples/communications/<section>/<script>.py``.

Sections
--------

- **decibels** -- the Bell System transmission unit.
- **propagation** -- Hertz's waves, the Friis transmission formula and the Telstar link budget.
- **noise** -- Johnson-Nyquist noise and Friis's cascaded noise factor.
- **modulation** -- Fessenden's AM, Carson's suppressed carrier and Armstrong's FM.
- **information** -- Nyquist's signalling rate, Hartley's law, Shannon's entropy and capacity.
- **detection** -- Kotel'nikov's optimum BPSK receiver and quadrature (QPSK) modulation.
- **coding** -- the Hamming(7,4) code.
- **multiplexing** -- DFT-based OFDM with a cyclic prefix.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Harry Nyquist&#x27;s &quot;Certain topics in telegraph transmission theory&quot; (1928) showed that a channel of bandwidth B can carry at most 2B independent pulses per second without intersymbol interference (~engineeringkit.communications.nyquist_symbol_rate). Sinc-shaped pulses spaced 1/2B apart are each zero at all the other sampling instants, so every pulse can be read without seeing its neighbours. Sending faster makes the pulses blur together.">

More levels raise the rate only logarithmically, and noise limits how many levels can be told apart. This example sends multilevel symbols through a noisy channel to show that limit.">

.. raw:: html

For a binary source entropy_binary peaks at one bit when both symbols are equally likely. The same paper defined channel capacity. A binary symmetric channel that flips each bit with probability p has capacity 1 - H(p) (~engineeringkit.communications.binary_symmetric_capacity): even a very noisy channel can carry information reliably at a reduced rate, and a channel with p = 1/2 carries nothing.">

Writing S/N = (E_b/N_0)(C/B) gives the minimum energy per bit for a given spectral efficiency. As bandwidth becomes unlimited the minimum tends to \ln 2, or -1.59 dB, the Shannon limit.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="On Christmas Eve 1906 Reginald Fessenden is said to have broadcast voice and music from Brant Rock, Massachusetts, by varying the amplitude of a continuous high-frequency wave. Conventional AM, s(t) = A_c[1+\mu m(t)]\cos 2\pi f_c t (~engineeringkit.communications.am_signal), keeps the carrier so a simple envelope detector can recover the message, provided the modulation index stays at or below one. .. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="In 1915 John Renshaw Carson of AT&amp;T showed that the carrier in AM carries no information. Multiplying the message by the carrier alone, s(t) = m(t)\cos 2\pi f_c t (~engineeringkit.communications.dsb_sc), puts all the transmitted power into the sidebands. The receiver must supply a coherent carrier: mix, low-pass filter, and the message returns, scaled by \tfrac12\cos\phi for a phase error \phi. Carson also showed that one sideband is enough, the basis of single-sideband telephony.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Robert Chang (1966) showed that subcarriers whose spectra overlap remain orthogonal if they are spaced by the symbol rate. Stephen Weinstein and Paul Ebert (1971) generated the whole set with a single inverse DFT (~engineeringkit.communications.ofdm_modulate) and prefixed each block with a copy of its tail. This cyclic prefix turns a dispersive channel&#x27;s linear convolution into a circular one, so after the receiver&#x27;s DFT (~engineeringkit.communications.ofdm_demodulate) every subcarrier sees one complex gain, removed by a single division. The idea now carries Wi-Fi, LTE and digital television.">

Johnson at Bell Labs measured a random voltage across every resistor, proportional to its resistance and absolute temperature and independent of its material. Harry Nyquist explained it from thermodynamics: the available noise power in a bandwidth B is kTB (~engineeringkit.communications.thermal_noise_power), -174 dBm/Hz at 290 K.
