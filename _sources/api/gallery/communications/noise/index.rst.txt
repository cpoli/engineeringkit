    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="In 1926-1928 John B. Johnson at Bell Labs measured a random voltage across every resistor, proportional to its resistance and absolute temperature and independent of its material. Harry Nyquist explained it from thermodynamics: the available noise power in a bandwidth B is kTB (~engineeringkit.communications.thermal_noise_power), -174 dBm/Hz at 290 K. This example synthesises white Gaussian noise with that power, measures it after band-limiting, and recovers the open-circuit voltage \sqrt{4kTRB}.">

Later stages are suppressed by the gain ahead of them, so a low-noise amplifier belongs first. The example checks the formula against a sample-by-sample simulation of noisy amplifiers and compares two orderings of the same parts.">
