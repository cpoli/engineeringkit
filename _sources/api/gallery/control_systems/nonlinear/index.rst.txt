.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Aleksandr Andronov&#x27;s school in Gorky studied self-sustained oscillations by eliminating time and drawing the motion of \ddot x = f(x, \dot x) as a curve in the plane (x, \dot x) (~engineeringkit.control_systems.phase_portrait). A relay servo, \ddot x = -M\,\mathrm{sign}(x), is not linear anywhere, yet its phase plane is read at a glance. Each half plane holds parabolas \dot x^2/2 + M|x| = E, which join into closed orbits: the servo oscillates for ever at the amplitude it started with. Feeding back the rate, \ddot x = -M\,\mathrm{sign}(x + \tau\dot x), tilts the switching line. Every orbit then loses energy at each switch, reaches the line and slides along it, \dot x = -x/\tau, into the origin.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Ralph Kochenburger, working on relay-driven propeller pitch controls, replaced a nonlinearity by the gain N(A) it offers to the fundamental of a sinusoid of amplitude A (~engineeringkit.control_systems.relay_describing_function, saturation_describing_function). A loop L(s) closed through it can then oscillate where L(j\omega) = -1/N(A): at a phase crossover, with N(A) equal to the gain margin (~engineeringkit.control_systems.limit_cycle). For an ideal relay N = 4M/(\pi A) and L = 1/(s+1)^3, the cycle is at \omega = \sqrt3 with A = M/(2\pi). This is also the relay experiment that autotuners use to find Ziegler and Nichols&#x27;s ultimate point. The method is approximate because the loop does not filter out the harmonics completely, so each prediction is checked against a simulation of the nonlinear loop.">
