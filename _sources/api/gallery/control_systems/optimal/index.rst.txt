Optimal control and estimation
------------------------------

The linear-quadratic regulator, the Kalman-Bucy filter and the robustness of their combination.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Rudolf Kalman (1960) asked for the state feedback u = -Kx that minimises J = \int_0^\infty (x^\mathsf{T}Qx + u^\mathsf{T}Ru)\,dt. The answer is K = R^{-1}B^\mathsf{T}P, where P solves an algebraic Riccati equation, and the minimum cost from x_0 is x_0^\mathsf{T}Px_0 (~engineeringkit.control_systems.lqr). The weights replace pole locations as the designer&#x27;s knobs: a cheaper input (smaller R) buys a faster response.">

It seemed natural that an LQR gain fed by a Kalman filter (the LQG controller) would inherit these margins. John Doyle&#x27;s 1978 note, whose abstract reads in full &quot;There are none.&quot;, gave a counterexample. As the design weights grow the LQG loop&#x27;s margins shrink towards zero, even though each half is optimal.">
