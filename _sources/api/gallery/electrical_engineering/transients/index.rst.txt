    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Before the first transatlantic cable was laid, William Thomson (later Lord Kelvin) analysed how a signal spreads along a submarine cable. In 1855 he showed that, with negligible inductance, the line obeys a diffusion equation: a pulse arrives smeared over a time proportional to rc\,\ell^2, the resistance and capacitance per unit length times the length squared, the law of squares. The building block is the single RC section, whose voltage rises as 1 - e^{-t/RC} (~engineeringkit.electrical_engineering.rc_charging_voltage). This example checks the time constant of one section, then chains sections into a cable and confirms the law of squares.">
