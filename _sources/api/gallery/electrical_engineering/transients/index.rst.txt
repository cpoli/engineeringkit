    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Before the first transatlantic cable was laid, William Thomson (later Lord Kelvin) analysed how a signal spreads along a submarine cable. In 1855 he showed that, with negligible inductance, the line obeys a diffusion equation: a pulse arrives smeared over a time proportional to rc\,\ell^2, the resistance and capacitance per unit length times the length squared, the law of squares. The building block is the single RC section, whose voltage rises as 1 - e^{-t/RC} (~engineeringkit.electrical_engineering.rc_charging_voltage). This example checks the time constant of one section, then chains sections into a cable and confirms the law of squares.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="When a battery is switched onto a coil, the current does not jump to Ohm&#x27;s value: the coil&#x27;s self-induction opposes the change. Hermann Helmholtz measured how long induced currents last and in 1851 gave the law of their rise, i = (V/R)\left(1 - e^{-Rt/L}\right), with the time scale L/R (~engineeringkit.electrical_engineering.rl_current, rl_time_constant). Opening the circuit, the current decays with the same time constant. This example switches a relay coil on and off and checks the energy bookkeeping: what the source supplies is dissipated in the resistance or stored as \tfrac12 L i^2.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Felix Savary and Joseph Henry had seen that a Leyden jar discharged through a coil could magnetise needles either way, as if the current reversed. In 1853 William Thomson (later Lord Kelvin) wrote down the equation of the discharge, a capacitance, an inductance and a resistance in series, and found that it is a single surge if R &gt; 2\sqrt{L/C} and an oscillation of period close to 2\pi\sqrt{LC} if it is less. Feddersen photographed the oscillating spark a few years later. In modern terms the series RLC circuit has the neper frequency \alpha = R/2L and the resonant frequency \omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC}, and is over-, critically or underdamped as \alpha is larger than, equal to or smaller than \omega_0 (~engineeringkit.electrical_engineering.rlc_damping, rlc_step_voltage). This example shows the three regimes of the step response and checks the ringing frequency and decay of the underdamped one.">
