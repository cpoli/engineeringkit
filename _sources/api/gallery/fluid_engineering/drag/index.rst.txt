    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="George Gabriel Stokes, correcting pendulum experiments for the air dragged along with the bob, solved the slow viscous flow past a sphere in a paper read to the Cambridge Philosophical Society in December 1850 and printed in 1851. The drag is 3\pi\mu dV (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.stokes_drag), so a small sphere settles at (\rho_p - \rho)gd^2/(18\mu) (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.stokes_settling_velocity). The law fails once inertia matters, near Re = 1; the measured drag curve (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.sphere_drag_coefficient) takes over. This example draws the curve and finds how fast sand grains of different sizes fall through water (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.terminal_velocity).">
