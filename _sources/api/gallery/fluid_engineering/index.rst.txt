Examples
========

.. include:: /_generated/nav/fluid_engineering.rst

This gallery walks through every public feature of
``engineeringkit.fluid_engineering``, one breakthrough per script:
hydrostatics, flow meters, pipe friction from Hagen and Poiseuille to Swamee
and Jain, ship models and dimensional analysis, open channels, water
hammer, pump cavitation and pipe networks.

:doc:`Units, assumptions and numerical validation </guides/fluid_engineering>`
gives the physical validity limits. Each script is self-contained and can be
run directly with ``python examples/fluid_engineering/<section>/<script>.py``.

Sections
--------

- **hydrostatics** -- Archimedes's principle and Stevin's hydrostatic paradox.
- **measurement** -- Pitot's tube and Venturi's meter.
- **pipe_flow** -- Hagen-Poiseuille, Darcy-Weisbach, Reynolds's transition, Nikuradse's roughness, Colebrook's equation, Moody's diagram and Swamee-Jain.
- **similarity** -- Froude's ship models and Buckingham's Pi theorem.
- **open_channel** -- Manning's formula.
- **transients** -- Joukowsky's water hammer.
- **pumps** -- Thoma's cavitation parameter.
- **networks** -- Hardy Cross's method for pipe networks.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="In On Floating Bodies (c. 250 BC) Archimedes proved that a body immersed in a fluid is pushed up by the weight of the fluid it displaces, F_B = \rho g V (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.buoyancy_force).     <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="In 1732 Henri Pitot lowered a glass tube bent at a right angle into the Seine, its mouth facing upstream, and watched the water inside rise above the river surface. The rise is the velocity head v^2/(2g), and it showed, against the belief of the day, that the current is faster near the surface than at depth. Turned around, the stagnation rise gives the speed, v = \sqrt{2\Delta p/\rho} (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.pitot_velocity). This example reads river and air speeds from a Pitot tube and finds the Mach number at which the incompressible formula starts to mislead an airspeed indicator.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="A city&#x27;s water mains form loops, and the flow in each pipe is fixed by two sets of conditions: continuity at every junction, and zero net head loss round every loop. In 1936 Hardy Cross, at the University of Illinois, proposed solving them by hand: guess flows that satisfy continuity, then correct one loop at a time by \Delta Q = -\sum r Q|Q|/(2\sum r|Q|), a Newton step that leaves the junctions balanced (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.hardy_cross). .. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="In 1868 William Froude proposed to the Admiralty that a ship&#x27;s resistance could be predicted from towing a small model, if the model ran at the &quot;corresponding speed&quot;, v_m = v_s\sqrt{L_m/L_s}. Then the waves the two make have the same shape, because the Froude number Fr = v/\sqrt{gL} (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.froude_number) is the same. Skin friction does not scale that way, so Froude split the resistance: friction from flat-plank tests, the rest scaled with the displacement. His tank at Torquay checked the method against full-scale towing of HMS Greyhound in 1871. This example scales a model test to a ship, using the ITTC 1957 friction line in place of Froude&#x27;s planks.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Burst mains in Moscow&#x27;s new water system led Nikolai Joukowsky to a series of experiments in 1897, with pipes up to 760 m long. He reported in 1898 that closing a valve suddenly raises the pressure by \Delta p = \rho a \Delta v (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.water_hammer_pressure), where a is the speed of the pressure wave, slowed below the speed of sound in water by the elasticity of the pipe wall (Korteweg&#x27;s formula, pipe_wave_speed). The wave runs to the reservoir and back in 2L/a (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.critical_closure_time), and a valve closed faster than that sees the full rise. This example compares pipe materials and draws the pressure at the valve.">
