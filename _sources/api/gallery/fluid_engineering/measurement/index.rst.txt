    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="In 1732 Henri Pitot lowered a glass tube bent at a right angle into the Seine, its mouth facing upstream, and watched the water inside rise above the river surface. The rise is the velocity head v^2/(2g), and it showed, against the belief of the day, that the current is faster near the surface than at depth. Turned around, the stagnation rise gives the speed, v = \sqrt{2\Delta p/\rho} (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.pitot_velocity). This example reads river and air speeds from a Pitot tube and finds the Mach number at which the incompressible formula starts to mislead an airspeed indicator.">

Clemens Herschel turned the effect into the Venturi water meter in 1887: continuity and Bernoulli&#x27;s equation give the flow from the pressure drop between inlet and throat, Q = C_d A_2\sqrt{2\Delta p/[\rho(1 - (A_2/A_1)^2)]} (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.venturi_flow). This example draws the pressure along a meter and reads the flow back from it.">
