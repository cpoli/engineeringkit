    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Lester Pelton, a millwright in the California gold country, patented in 1880 a wheel whose buckets split the jet in two and turn each half almost back on itself. By the momentum equation a vane that turns a jet through \theta is pushed with \rho A (V - u)^2 (1 - \cos\theta) (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.jet_force); a wheel, which always has a bucket in the jet, takes \rho Q u (V - u)(1 - \cos\theta) (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.pelton_wheel_power). Turning the jet through nearly 180° and running the buckets at half the jet speed leaves the water with almost no speed at all. This example compares Pelton&#x27;s buckets with the flat paddles of the hurdy-gurdy wheels they replaced.">
