    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="A city&#x27;s water mains form loops, and the flow in each pipe is fixed by two sets of conditions: continuity at every junction, and zero net head loss round every loop. In 1936 Hardy Cross, at the University of Illinois, proposed solving them by hand: guess flows that satisfy continuity, then correct one loop at a time by \Delta Q = -\sum r Q|Q|/(2\sum r|Q|), a Newton step that leaves the junctions balanced (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.hardy_cross). It was the standard method until computers took over. This example balances a two-loop network sized with Darcy-Weisbach resistances (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.pipe_resistance).">
