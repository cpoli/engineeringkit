    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Burst mains in Moscow&#x27;s new water system led Nikolai Joukowsky to a series of experiments in 1897, with pipes up to 760 m long. He reported in 1898 that closing a valve suddenly raises the pressure by \Delta p = \rho a \Delta v (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.water_hammer_pressure), where a is the speed of the pressure wave, slowed below the speed of sound in water by the elasticity of the pipe wall (Korteweg&#x27;s formula, pipe_wave_speed). The wave runs to the reservoir and back in 2L/a (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.critical_closure_time), and a valve closed faster than that sees the full rise. This example compares pipe materials and draws the pressure at the valve.">
