    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Maurice Couette, in his 1890 thesis at Paris, measured the viscosity of water in the narrow gap between a fixed inner cylinder and a rotating outer one, from the torque on the inner cylinder. Rotating the outer cylinder keeps the flow laminar to high speeds. For a narrow gap the flow is the plane shear flow between a fixed and a sliding plate, with a straight velocity profile and uniform stress \mu U/h (~engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.couette_velocity, couette_shear_stress); the exact torque for any gap is couette_viscometer_torque. This example reads a viscosity back from a torque and adds a pressure gradient to the plane flow.">
