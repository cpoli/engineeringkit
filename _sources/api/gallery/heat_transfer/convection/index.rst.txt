Convection
----------

Newton cooling and Nusselt-number correlations for forced and natural convection.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="In 1701 Newton published, anonymously, a temperature scale reaching far beyond the melting point of tin. He heated a block of iron red hot, set it in a steady wind, and timed how long it took to cool. Assuming that the heat lost is proportional to the excess temperature, \dot Q = hA(T - T_\infty) (~engineeringkit.heat_transfer.convection_heat_rate), equal intervals of time should multiply the excess temperature by equal factors (~engineeringkit.heat_transfer.lumped_temperature). Newton then read temperatures off the clock. This example repeats the measurement on a model block, recovers the time constant from the readings, and checks the energy balance.">

In McAdams&#x27;s rounded form, Nu = 0.023\,Re^{0.8}Pr^{n} with n = 0.4 for heating and 0.3 for cooling (~engineeringkit.heat_transfer.dittus_boelter_nusselt), it became the most quoted equation in convection. This example sizes the coefficient for water in a tube, shows that the Colburn analogy between friction and heat transfer reproduces the 0.023, and measures the scatter against Gnielinski&#x27;s modern correlation.">

In 1975 Stuart Churchill and Humbert Chu fitted one smooth expression to the data from conduction-dominated creeping flow to fully turbulent plumes, Nu = \{0.825 + 0.387 Ra^{1/6}/[1 + (0.492/Pr)^{9/16}]^{8/27}\}^2 (~engineeringkit.heat_transfer.churchill_chu_nusselt, with rayleigh_number).
