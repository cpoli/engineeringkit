    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="The log-mean temperature difference sizes an exchanger when all four end temperatures are known (~engineeringkit.heat_transfer.log_mean_temperature_difference, heat_exchanger_rate). To rate a given exchanger, when only the inlets are known, it needs iteration. William Kays and Alexander London, testing compact exchangers for the US Navy at Stanford, organised everything around the effectiveness \varepsilon = \dot Q/\dot Q_{max} as a function of NTU = UA/C_{min} and C_r = C_{min}/C_{max} (~engineeringkit.heat_transfer.exchanger_effectiveness_counterflow, exchanger_effectiveness_parallel), and published the charts in Compact Heat Exchangers (1955). This example rates an oil cooler both ways and marches the energy equations along it.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="The log-mean temperature difference is exact only for pure counterflow or parallel flow. In a shell-and-tube exchanger the tube fluid passes back and forth while the shell fluid crosses it, and in a crossflow core neither stream sees a single temperature. R. A. Bowman, A. C. Mueller and W. M. Nagle, collecting the analyses of the 1930s, presented the correction as one factor F on the counterflow LMTD, charted against the effectiveness P of one stream and the capacity ratio R (~engineeringkit.heat_transfer.lmtd_correction_factor). The effectiveness-NTU forms of the same arrangements (~engineeringkit.heat_transfer.exchanger_effectiveness_shell_tube, exchanger_effectiveness_crossflow) carry the same information. This example redraws the chart, checks the two methods against each other and rates a two-shell oil cooler.">
