.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Thomas Young&#x27;s Lectures on Natural Philosophy (1807) gave a single number for the stiffness of a material, its &quot;modulus of elasticity&quot;. Young expressed it as the height of a column of the material whose weight would compress it to nothing if the law stayed linear; Navier (1826) restated it as the ratio of stress to strain, E = \sigma/\varepsilon, the form used today (~engineeringkit.materials.hooke_stress). The example reduces a simulated tensile test from load and extension to stress and strain (~engineeringkit.materials.normal_stress, normal_strain), fits the modulus with StressStrainCurve and measures the energy the bar absorbs.">

The stress does not depend on the length of the bar, which is why continuously welded rail must be laid at a chosen neutral temperature.">
