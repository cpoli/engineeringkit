Olin Basquin (1910) noticed that on log-log axes the finite-life part of Wöhler&#x27;s curves is a straight line, \sigma_a = \sigma_f&#x27;(2N)^b (~engineeringkit.materials.basquin_cycles). The example fits the line to scattered test results with power_law_fit and recovers the constants that produced them.">

Arvid Palmgren (1924), estimating the lives of ball bearings, assumed that each cycle at amplitude \sigma_i uses up a fraction 1/N_i of the life, and Milton Miner (1945) confirmed the rule on riveted aluminium joints for aircraft: failure comes when D = \sum n_i/N_i reaches 1 (~engineeringkit.materials.miner_damage). The example applies it to a block load spectrum and reduces the spectrum to one equivalent amplitude.">

Louis Coffin (1954) and Samson Manson (1953-1954) independently found that the plastic strain amplitude follows its own power law, \Delta\varepsilon_p/2 = \varepsilon_f&#x27;(2N)^c (~engineeringkit.materials.coffin_manson_cycles). Added to Basquin&#x27;s elastic line it gives the strain-life curve (~engineeringkit.materials.strain_life_amplitude) used for notched parts today.">
