    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Christiaan Huygens (1673) asked how long a simple pendulum must be to swing in step with a rigid body pivoted at a point. His answer, the length L = I_O/(m d) to the centre of oscillation, needs the body&#x27;s moment of inertia about the pivot, which Jakob Steiner later wrote in general as I_O = I_G + m d^2 (~engineeringkit.mechanical_engineering.parallel_axis). The example checks the theorem by summing r^2\,dm over a disk, recovers Huygens&#x27;s rule that pivot and centre of oscillation can be swapped without changing the period, and finds the correction he applied to a clock&#x27;s spherical bob.">
