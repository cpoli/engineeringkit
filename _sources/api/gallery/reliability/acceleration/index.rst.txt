Accelerated life testing
------------------------

Carrying lives measured under high stress back to service conditions.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Svante Arrhenius (1889), measuring how fast acid inverts cane sugar, found that reaction rates grow with temperature as e^{-E_a/(k_B T)}. Many failure mechanisms of electronics, such as diffusion, corrosion and intermetallic growth, are reactions, so a part&#x27;s life at temperature T scales as e^{E_a/(k_B T)}. A test at 125 °C then stands in for years at 55 °C, through the acceleration factor (~engineeringkit.reliability.arrhenius_acceleration_factor). Lives measured at several temperatures fix E_a (~engineeringkit.reliability.arrhenius_fit).">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Henry Eyring (1935) derived reaction rates from statistical mechanics: reactants pass through an activated complex, and the rate is (k_B T/h)\,e^{-\Delta G^\ddagger/(k_B T)}. The prefactor T is absent from Arrhenius&#x27;s empirical law. For life testing it means L \propto T^{-1}e^{E_a/(k_B T)}, and an acceleration factor T_t/T_u times the Arrhenius one (~engineeringkit.reliability.eyring_acceleration_factor). The model extends naturally to non-thermal stresses such as voltage, here represented by the inverse power law (~engineeringkit.reliability.inverse_power_law_acceleration_factor).">
