.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Where should the i-th of n failures be plotted? The population fraction failed at the i-th smallest life is itself random, with a \mathrm{Beta}(i, n - i + 1) distribution. Leonard Johnson (1951), working on bearing fatigue at General Motors, proposed its median, the median rank, as the plotting position, and Benard&#x27;s (i - 0.3)/(n + 0.4) approximates it closely (~engineeringkit.reliability.median_ranks). For censored data Johnson&#x27;s adjusted order numbers (~engineeringkit.reliability.adjusted_ranks) let units removed early still move the later ranks. A least-squares line on Weibull axes then gives \beta and \eta (~engineeringkit.reliability.weibull_rank_regression).">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="A rank-regression line uses a censored unit only through the ranks of later failures. A. Clifford Cohen (1965) wrote out the maximum-likelihood equations of the Weibull distribution for complete, singly censored and progressively censored samples. Every unit contributes: a failure through its density, a survivor through R(t). The shape solves one equation in \beta alone, and \eta follows (~engineeringkit.reliability.weibull_mle). The example repeats a heavily censored test many times and compares the scatter of the two estimators.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Howard Raiffa and Robert Schlaifer (1961) named and catalogued conjugate priors, families that a likelihood maps into themselves. For failures that arrive as a Poisson process the conjugate family is the gamma distribution: a \mathrm{Gamma}(a, b) prior on the rate \lambda and r failures in time T give a \mathrm{Gamma}(a + r, b + T) posterior (~engineeringkit.reliability.bayesian_failure_rate). Handbook or similar-part data set the prior (~engineeringkit.reliability.gamma_prior_from_bounds), so a short test of a new part is not read in isolation.">
