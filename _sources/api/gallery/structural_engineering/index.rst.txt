Examples
========

.. include:: /_generated/nav/structural_engineering.rst

This gallery walks through every public feature of
``engineeringkit.structural_engineering``, one breakthrough per script:
axial and torsional members, beams, column buckling and design, stress and
strain at a point, shear and sections, pressure vessels, energy methods,
trusses, plastic collapse, frames, cables and arches.

:doc:`Units, assumptions and numerical validation </guides/structural_engineering>`
gives the physical validity limits. Each script is self-contained and can be
run directly with ``python examples/structural_engineering/<section>/<script>.py``.

Sections
--------

- **beams** -- Galileo's cantilever, Navier's bending theory, Clapeyron's three-moment equation, Winkler's influence lines and Macaulay's singularity functions.
- **cables** -- Hooke's inverted chain and the catenary.
- **members** -- Hooke's law, Coulomb's torsion, Poisson's ratio and Bredt's thin-walled tubes.
- **shear** -- Jourawski's shear stress and Maillart's shear centre.
- **stability** -- Euler's column, Engesser's tangent-modulus load, Young's eccentric column and Johnson's parabola.
- **stress** -- Mohr's circle and the bonded strain gauge.
- **vessels** -- Lamé's thick-walled cylinder.
- **energy** -- Castigliano's theorem and Mohr's unit-load method.
- **trusses** -- Whipple's method of joints, Maxwell's count and reciprocal theorem, and the direct stiffness method.
- **plasticity** -- Kazinczy's plastic hinges.
- **frames** -- Maney's slope-deflection, Hardy Cross's moment distribution and plane frames by direct stiffness.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Robert Hooke hid his solution to the shape of an arch in a Latin anagram at the end of A Description of Helioscopes (1675); decoded after his death, it read ut pendet continuum flexile, sic stabit contiguum rigidum inversum: as hangs the flexible line, so but inverted will stand the rigid arch. For a three-hinged arch (~engineeringkit.structural_engineering.three_hinged_arch) the moment is the simple-beam moment less the thrust times the rise, M = M_0 - Hy, and on a parabolic arch under a load uniform along the span it vanishes everywhere. The example checks this, then moves the load off the crown to show the bending that an arch of the wrong shape must carry.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Continuous beams and building frames of the 1920s led to large systems of simultaneous equations that no engineer wanted to solve by hand. In 1930 Hardy Cross proposed relaxation instead. Lock every joint against rotation and apply the fixed-end moments. Then release one joint at a time: its unbalanced moment is shared among the members meeting there in proportion to their stiffness 4EI/L, and half of each share is carried over to the far end of the member. Repeat until the joints balance (~engineeringkit.structural_engineering.moment_distribution). The method fitted on a sheet of paper and was used for forty years. The example balances a three-span beam built in at its left end, checks the result against the plane-frame stiffness solution and plots how fast the unbalance dies away.">

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="American railway bridges of the 1840s were built from rules of thumb, and some fell down. Squire Whipple, in A Work on Bridge Building (1847), showed that the force in every member of a pin-jointed truss follows from statics alone. Each joint is in equilibrium, so its two equations, \sum F_x = 0 and \sum F_y = 0, give the forces in two members. Start at a support and work across the truss, two unknowns at a time (~engineeringkit.structural_engineering.method_of_joints). The example solves a six-panel Pratt truss under a train of panel loads and checks the chord forces against the method of sections: at any vertical cut, the chord force is the bending moment divided by the truss depth.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="James Clerk Maxwell&#x27;s short 1864 paper &quot;On the calculation of the equilibrium and stiffness of frames&quot; made two contributions. The count is necessary but not sufficient, since bars can be placed where they are not needed.
