    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="In 1914 Gábor Kazinczy loaded steel beams built into concrete walls in Budapest. They carried far more than the elastic theory allowed: when the most stressed section yielded through its whole depth it went on turning at a constant plastic moment M_p = \sigma_yZ_p (a plastic hinge), and the load moved to other sections until enough hinges had formed to make a mechanism. For a rectangle Z_p = bh^2/4, 1.5 times the elastic modulus (~engineeringkit.structural_engineering.plastic_section_modulus). The collapse load follows from the work equation of the mechanism (~engineeringkit.structural_engineering.plastic_collapse_load). The example follows a built-in beam under a uniform load from first yield, through hinges at the walls, to collapse with a third hinge at midspan.">
