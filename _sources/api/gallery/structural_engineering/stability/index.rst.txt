    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="In an appendix on elastic curves to his Methodus inveniendi (1744), Leonhard Euler found the load at which a straight, pin-ended column can stay in equilibrium bent as well as straight: P_{cr} = \pi^2EI/L^2. Other end conditions change the length of the half sine wave, which is what the effective-length factor K expresses (~engineeringkit.structural_engineering.euler_buckling_load). Dividing by the area gives the Euler stress \pi^2E/(KL/r)^2, a function of the slenderness alone (~engineeringkit.structural_engineering.slenderness_ratio, radius_of_gyration). The example recovers Euler&#x27;s load as the smallest eigenvalue of the finite-difference column equation EIy&#x27;&#x27; + Py = 0, then plots the Euler hyperbola against the yield stress.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Tests on real columns of intermediate length failed well below Euler&#x27;s load. Friedrich Engesser (1889) argued that once the stress passes the proportional limit, a column bends against the material&#x27;s tangent modulus E_t = d\sigma/d\varepsilon and not against E, so P_t = \pi^2E_tI/(KL)^2. Since E_t depends on the stress it creates, the buckling stress solves \sigma = \pi^2E_t(\sigma)/(KL/r)^2 (~engineeringkit.structural_engineering.tangent_modulus_load). Engesser later proposed a &quot;double-modulus&quot; load, and Shanley (1947) showed that the tangent-modulus load is the one at which a real column starts to bend. The example builds the column curve of an aluminium alloy with a Ramberg-Osgood stress-strain law and checks the computed stresses against the defining equation.">

Johnson and his co-authors, in The Theory and Practice of Modern Framed Structures (1893), fitted a parabola that starts at the yield stress and meets Euler&#x27;s curve tangentially at half of it (~engineeringkit.structural_engineering.johnson_critical_stress), at the transition slenderness C_c = \sqrt{2\pi^2E/\sigma_y}. The AISC column formula used the same parabola for decades. End conditions enter through the effective length KL (~engineeringkit.structural_engineering.effective_length_factor). The example draws the design curve and checks that the two branches meet with the same slope.">
