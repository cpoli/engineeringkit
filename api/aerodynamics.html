engineeringkit.aerodynamics#

Domain hub · History · Examples · API reference

Dynamic pressure and force coefficients; Kutta-Joukowski circulation lift; thin-airfoil theory (with Glauert’s zero-lift integral by scipy.integrate.quad) and NACA four-digit sections; Prandtl’s lifting line, induced drag, the Oswald drag polar and the Breguet range equation; Reynolds similarity and Blasius/Prandtl flat-plate friction; sound speed, the Mach cone, isentropic stagnation states, Prandtl-Glauert, normal shocks and the area-Mach relation; and the International Standard Atmosphere. All models are hand-written closed forms, because the formula is the subject.

engineeringkit.aerodynamics: from dynamic pressure to finite wings and compressible flow.

Force coefficients and dynamic pressure; Kutta-Joukowski circulation lift; thin-airfoil theory and NACA four-digit sections; Prandtl’s lifting line, induced drag, the parabolic drag polar and Breguet range; Reynolds-number similarity and Blasius/Prandtl flat-plate boundary layers; perfect-gas sound speed, isentropic stagnation states, the Mach cone, Prandtl-Glauert, Rankine-Hugoniot normal shocks and the de Laval area-Mach relation; and the International Standard Atmosphere. Every model is a hand-written closed-form relation (the formula is the pedagogical subject); SciPy is used only for the thin-airfoil quadrature.

engineeringkit.aerodynamics.breguet_range(V, tsfc, lift_to_drag, W_initial, W_final, g=9.80665)[source]#

Jet-aircraft cruise range \(R = \dfrac{V}{g\,c_T}\dfrac{L}{D}\ln\dfrac{W_i}{W_f}\) (Breguet).

Integrates \(dR = V\,dt\) while fuel burns at \(\dot m = c_T T\) and thrust equals drag, \(T = W/(L/D)\), at constant speed and lift-to-drag ratio.

Parameters:

  • V (float or array_like) – Cruise true airspeed [m/s], positive.

  • tsfc (float or array_like) – Thrust-specific fuel consumption [kg/(N s)], positive (e.g. 1.6e-5 for a modern turbofan).

  • lift_to_drag (float or array_like) – Cruise lift-to-drag ratio [-], positive.

  • W_initial (float or array_like) – Weight at the start and end of cruise [N], with W_initial >= W_final > 0.

  • W_final (float or array_like) – Weight at the start and end of cruise [N], with W_initial >= W_final > 0.

  • g (float, optional) – Gravitational acceleration [m/s^2].

Returns:

Still-air range [m].

Return type:

float or ndarray

Notes

Inputs are raw SI numbers; see Aerodynamics: incompressible limits and compressible conservation.

References

J. D. Anderson, Aircraft Performance and Design (McGraw-Hill, 1999), sec. 5.13, for the derivation of the range equation attributed to Louis Breguet.

Examples

engineeringkit.aerodynamics.naca4_airfoil(designation, n=101, closed_trailing_edge=False)[source]#

Coordinates of a NACA four-digit section on a unit chord.

The half-thickness is

\[y_t = 5t\,(0.2969\sqrt{x} - 0.1260x - 0.3516x^2 + 0.2843x^3 - 0.1015x^4),\]

and the surfaces are offset from the camber line along its normal. Stations are cosine-spaced to resolve the leading edge.

Parameters:

  • designation (str) – Four digits, e.g. "0012" or "2412".

  • n (int) – Number of chordwise stations, at least 3.

  • closed_trailing_edge (bool) – Use -0.1036 instead of -0.1015 for the quartic coefficient so the trailing edge has zero thickness.

Return type:

Airfoil

Notes

Coordinates are fractions of chord; see Aerodynamics: incompressible limits and compressible conservation.

References

E. N. Jacobs, K. E. Ward and R. M. Pinkerton, “The characteristics of 78 related airfoil sections from tests in the variable-density wind tunnel,” NACA Report 460 (1933).

Examples

>>> foil = naca4_airfoil("2412")
>>> round(foil.summary()["max_thickness"], 3)

engineeringkit.aerodynamics.naca4_zero_lift_angle(m, p)[source]#

Thin-airfoil zero-lift angle of a NACA four-digit camber line.

Glauert’s integral

\[\alpha_{L=0} = -\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \frac{dz}{dx}(\theta)\,(\cos\theta - 1)\,d\theta, \qquad x = \tfrac12(1-\cos\theta),\]

Parameters:

  • p (float) – Position of maximum camber as a fraction of chord, in (0, 1) (second digit / 10).

Returns:

Zero-lift angle of attack [rad]; zero for m = 0.

Return type:

float

Notes

Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Aerodynamics: incompressible limits and compressible conservation.

  • Clmax (float or array_like) – Maximum lift coefficient [-], positive.

Returns:

Stall speed [m/s].

Notes
