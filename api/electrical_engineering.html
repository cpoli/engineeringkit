engineeringkit.electrical_engineering: circuits from Ohm’s law to the digital load flow.

Ohm’s law, series and parallel equivalents, the Wheatstone bridge, modified nodal analysis and mesh analysis of resistive networks; Thévenin, Norton and Jacobi’s maximum power transfer; RC and RL transients and the over-, critically and underdamped RLC step; complex impedance, series resonance and the ideal transformer; ideal op-amp circuits, Shockley’s diode equation and the load line; DC machine and induction motor torque-speed curves; lossless and lossy transmission lines, Heaviside’s distortionless condition, reflections and input impedance for the Smith chart; and real, reactive and three-phase power, the per-unit system, Fortescue’s symmetrical components and fault currents, Gauss-Seidel and Newton-Raphson load flow, and the swing equation with the equal-area criterion. Models are hand-written; NumPy solves the linear systems and SciPy computes the RLC step response, integrals, roots and swing curves.

engineeringkit.electrical_engineering.equal_area_clearing_angle(p_mech, p_max_pre, p_max_fault, p_max_post)[source]#

Initial and critical clearing rotor angles by the equal-area criterion.

The rotor starts at \(\delta_0 = \arcsin(P_m/P_1)\) on the prefault curve, accelerates along the fault curve \(P_2\sin\delta\) and, once cleared, decelerates along \(P_3\sin\delta\) until at most \(\delta_{max} = \pi - \arcsin(P_m/P_3)\). Equating the accelerating and decelerating areas,

\[\cos\delta_{cr} = \frac{P_m(\delta_{max} - \delta_0) + P_3\cos\delta_{max} - P_2\cos\delta_0}{P_3 - P_2}.\]
Parameters:

  • p_mech (float or array_like) – Mechanical input power \(P_m\) [pu], positive.

  • p_max_pre (float or array_like) – Amplitudes \(P_1, P_2, P_3\) of the prefault, fault-on and post-fault power-angle curves [pu]; \(P_1, P_3 > P_m\) and \(0 \le P_2 < P_3\).

  • p_max_fault (float or array_like) – Amplitudes \(P_1, P_2, P_3\) of the prefault, fault-on and post-fault power-angle curves [pu]; \(P_1, P_3 > P_m\) and \(0 \le P_2 < P_3\).

  • p_max_post (float or array_like) – Amplitudes \(P_1, P_2, P_3\) of the prefault, fault-on and post-fault power-angle curves [pu]; \(P_1, P_3 > P_m\) and \(0 \le P_2 < P_3\).

Returns:

(delta0, delta_critical) [rad]. If the fault-on curve keeps the rotor from passing \(\delta_{max}\) whenever it is cleared, delta_critical is \(\delta_{max}\).

Return type:

tuple of float or ndarray

Raises:

ValueError – For inconsistent powers, or if the post-fault system cannot hold the rotor even when the fault is cleared at once.

Notes

Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Electrical engineering: passive components and circuit responses. Classical model: constant voltage behind transient reactance, constant mechanical power, no damping, one machine against an infinite bus.

References

R. D. Evans and C. F. Wagner, “Studies of transmission stability,” Trans. AIEE 45 (1926); E. W. Kimbark, Power System Stability, vol. 1 (New York: Wiley, 1948), ch. 4.

Examples

>>> d0, dcr = equal_area_clearing_angle(1.0, 2.0, 0.0, 2.0)
>>> round(float(np.degrees(d0)), 2), round(float(np.degrees(dcr)), 2)
(30.0, 79.56)
engineeringkit.electrical_engineering.fault_currents(e_prefault, z1, z2, z0, kind='slg', z_fault=0.0)[source]#

Phase fault currents at a bus from its sequence impedances.

The fault’s boundary conditions connect the Thévenin sequence networks seen from the faulted bus (phase a is the reference phase; a line-to-line fault is between phases b and c):

  • "3ph", balanced three-phase: \(I_1 = E/(Z_1 + Z_f)\), \(I_0 = I_2 = 0\);

  • "slg", single line (a) to ground: the three networks in series, \(I_0 = I_1 = I_2 = E/(Z_0 + Z_1 + Z_2 + 3Z_f)\);

  • "ll", line to line: positive and negative networks in parallel opposition, \(I_1 = -I_2 = E/(Z_1 + Z_2 + Z_f)\), \(I_0 = 0\);

  • "llg", double line to ground: the negative network in parallel with the zero network plus \(3Z_f\).

The phase currents then follow from phase_components().

Parameters:

  • e_prefault (complex or array_like) – Prefault (Thévenin) voltage at the bus [pu or V], nonzero.

  • z1 (complex or array_like) – Positive-, negative- and zero-sequence Thévenin impedances at the bus [pu or ohm].

  • z2 (complex or array_like) – Positive-, negative- and zero-sequence Thévenin impedances at the bus [pu or ohm].

  • z0 (complex or array_like) – Positive-, negative- and zero-sequence Thévenin impedances at the bus [pu or ohm].

  • kind ({"slg", "ll", "llg", "3ph"}, optional) – Fault type.

  • z_fault (complex or array_like, optional) – Fault impedance [pu or ohm] (to ground for "slg" and "llg", between the faulted lines for "ll", per phase for "3ph").

Returns:

(Ia, Ib, Ic), phase currents into the fault [pu or A].

Return type:

tuple of complex or ndarray

Raises:

ValueError – For an unknown kind or a fault path of zero total impedance.

Notes

Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Electrical engineering: passive components and circuit responses. Use one consistent unit system (per unit, or volts and ohms) for all inputs. Load current before the fault is neglected.

References

C. L. Fortescue, “Method of symmetrical co-ordinates applied to the solution of polyphase networks,” Trans. AIEE 37 (1918) 1027-1140; J. D. Glover, T. J. Overbye and M. S. Sarma, Power System Analysis and Design, 6th ed. (Boston: Cengage, 2017), ch. 7 and 9.

engineeringkit.electrical_engineering.gauss_seidel_load_flow(Y, p_load, q_load, v_slack=1.0, tol=1e-08, max_iter=500, acceleration=1.0)[source]#

Gauss-Seidel load flow for a slack bus and PQ buses.

Bus 0 is the slack bus with fixed voltage. At every other bus the net injected complex power \(S_i = P_i + jQ_i\) is specified, and each sweep updates the bus voltages in turn,

\[V_i \leftarrow \frac{1}{Y_{ii}}\left(\frac{S_i^*}{V_i^*} - \sum_{k \ne i} Y_{ik} V_k\right),\]

using the newest value of every \(V_k\).

Parameters:

  • Y (array_like of complex) – Bus admittance matrix [pu], square, at least 2x2, nonzero diagonal; see admittance_matrix().

  • p_load (array_like) – Real [pu] and reactive [pu] power consumed at buses 1..n-1 (length n - 1); negative values are generation.

  • q_load (array_like) – Real [pu] and reactive [pu] power consumed at buses 1..n-1 (length n - 1); negative values are generation.

  • v_slack (complex, optional) – Slack-bus voltage [pu], nonzero.

  • tol (float, optional) – Stop when the largest voltage update is below this [pu], positive.

  • max_iter (int, optional) – Maximum number of sweeps, at least one.

  • acceleration (float, optional) – Over-relaxation factor [-] in \((0, 2)\); 1.4 to 1.6 is typical, 1 is plain Gauss-Seidel.

Returns:

Bus voltages, injected powers (bus 0 gives the slack power), sweep count, a convergence flag and the update history. No exception is raised on non-convergence; check converged.

Return type:

LoadFlowResult

Notes

Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Electrical engineering: passive components and circuit responses. PV (voltage-controlled) buses are not modelled.

References

J. B. Ward and H. W. Hale, “Digital computer solution of power-flow problems,” Trans. AIEE Part III 75 (1956) 398-404.

Examples

>>> Y = admittance_matrix(2, [(0, 1, 0.1j)])
>>> r = gauss_seidel_load_flow(Y, [0.5], [0.0])
>>> r.converged, round(float(abs(r.voltages[1])), 4)

engineeringkit.electrical_engineering.induction_motor_breakdown(v_phase, synchronous_speed, r1, x1, r2, x2, xm, phases=3)[source]#

Slip and torque at the breakdown (pull-out) point of an induction motor.

Torque is greatest when the rotor resistance \(R_2/s\) matches the impedance in front of it, at

\[s_{max} = \frac{R_2}{\sqrt{R_{th}^2 + (X_{th} + X_2)^2}},\qquad T_{max} = \frac{m V_{th}^2}{2\omega_s\left[R_{th} + \sqrt{R_{th}^2 + (X_{th} + X_2)^2}\right]},\]

so the breakdown torque does not depend on \(R_2\), only where it occurs.

Parameters:

  • v_phase (float or array_like) – RMS phase voltage [V], nonnegative.

  • synchronous_speed (float or array_like) – Synchronous shaft speed [rad/s], positive.

  • q_load (array_like) – Real [pu] and reactive [pu] power consumed at buses 1..n-1 (length n - 1); negative values are generation. q_load is ignored at PV buses, whose reactive power is an output.

  • tol (float, optional) – Stop when the largest power mismatch is below this [pu], positive.

  • max_iter (int, optional) – Maximum number of Newton steps, at least one.

engineeringkit.electrical_engineering.rlc_damping(R, L, C)[source]#

Neper frequency, resonant frequency and damping regime of a series RLC circuit.

The natural response of a series RLC circuit has the characteristic roots \(s = -\alpha \pm \sqrt{\alpha^2 - \omega_0^2}\) with \(\alpha = R/2L\) and \(\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC}\). It is overdamped for \(\alpha > \omega_0\), critically damped for \(\alpha = \omega_0\) (\(R = 2\sqrt{L/C}\)) and underdamped, an oscillation decaying as \(e^{-\alpha t}\), for \(\alpha < \omega_0\).

Parameters:

  • R (float) – Series resistance [ohm], nonnegative.

  • L (float) – Inductance [H], positive.

  • C (float) – Capacitance [F], positive.

Returns:

(alpha, omega0, regime): the neper frequency [1/s], the undamped resonant frequency [rad/s] and one of "overdamped", "critically damped" (within a relative 1e-9), "underdamped" or "undamped" (\(R = 0\)).

Return type:

tuple of (float, float, str)

Notes

Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Electrical engineering: passive components and circuit responses. The damping ratio is \(\zeta = \alpha/\omega_0\). R, L and C are scalar-only: the regime names one circuit.

References

W. Thomson, “On transient electric currents,” Phil. Mag. 5 (1853) 393-405.

Examples
