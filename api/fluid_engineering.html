engineeringkit.fluid_engineering#

Domain hub · History · Examples · API reference

Hydrostatic pressure, buoyancy and the force and centre of pressure on submerged planes; Bernoulli’s equation and continuity, with Pitot and Venturi meters; Reynolds number, Hagen-Poiseuille flow and its velocity profile, Darcy-Weisbach friction and minor losses, and the smooth, Nikuradse fully rough, Colebrook (scipy.optimize.brentq) and Swamee-Jain friction factors; Froude, Weber, Euler and Mach numbers and Buckingham’s Pi theorem by exact rational row reduction; Manning’s open-channel formula; Joukowsky’s water hammer with Korteweg’s wave speed; pump power, specific speed, NPSH and Thoma’s cavitation parameter; and Hardy Cross’s balancing of looped pipe networks.

engineeringkit.fluid_engineering: hydrostatics, momentum, pipe flow, drag, similarity, open channels, pumps and networks.

Stevin’s hydrostatic pressure and the force on submerged surfaces, Archimedes’s buoyancy; Bernoulli’s equation and continuity, with Pitot and Venturi meters, Torricelli’s orifice and sharp-crested weirs; the momentum equation for jets, bends and Pelton’s wheel; Couette flow and Stokes’s drag with the sphere drag curve; Reynolds’s regimes, Hagen-Poiseuille laminar flow, Darcy-Weisbach losses and the turbulent friction factors of Nikuradse, Colebrook and Swamee-Jain; Froude, Euler, Weber and Mach numbers and Buckingham’s Pi theorem by exact rational row reduction; Manning’s open-channel formula, specific energy, critical depth and Bélanger’s hydraulic jump; Joukowsky’s water hammer with Korteweg’s wave speed; Euler’s turbomachine head, pump and system curves, the affinity laws, pump power, specific speed, NPSH and Thoma’s cavitation parameter; and Hardy Cross’s balancing of looped pipe networks. Models are hand-written closed forms; SciPy supplies root bracketing.

engineeringkit.fluid_engineering.buckingham_pi(variables, repeating=None)[source]#

Dimensionless groups of a set of variables by Buckingham’s Pi theorem.

The variables’ dimensions form a matrix \(D\) (base dimensions by variables). Every vector \(x\) with \(Dx = 0\) lists the exponents of one dimensionless product, and a basis of that null space has \(n - \operatorname{rank} D\) vectors. The repeating variables are placed first, so the exact row reduction pivots on them and each group contains exactly one other variable, raised to a positive power.

Parameters:

  • variables (mapping) – Variable name to its dimensions, itself a mapping from a base dimension ("M", "L", "T", "Θ", …) to an integer exponent; for example {"rho": {"M": 1, "L": -3}}. Dimensionless variables map to {}.

  • repeating (sequence of str, optional) – Names of the repeating variables, as many as the rank of the dimension matrix and dimensionally independent. Defaults to the first independent variables in variables order.

Returns:

Variables, dimension matrix and the integer exponents of each group.

Return type:

PiGroups

Notes

Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Fluid engineering: flow regimes and signed quantities. The exponents come from exact rational arithmetic (integer_null_space()). The theorem fixes how many groups there are, not which ones: another choice of repeating variables gives products of these.

References

E. Buckingham, “On physically similar systems; illustrations of the use of dimensional equations,” Phys. Rev. 4 (1914) 345-376.

Examples

>>> pi = buckingham_pi(
...     {
...         "rho": {"M": 1, "L": -3},
...         "V": {"L": 1, "T": -1},
...         "D": {"L": 1},
...         "mu": {"M": 1, "L": -1, "T": -1},
...     }
... )
>>> pi.groups()
['rho^-1 V^-1 D^-1 mu^1']
