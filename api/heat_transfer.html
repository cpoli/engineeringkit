engineeringkit.heat_transfer#

Domain hub · History · Examples · API reference

Fourier’s law and thermal resistances (plane, cylindrical and spherical walls, composite walls, critical insulation radius); Harper-Brown fins; finite-difference conduction by the explicit, implicit and Crank-Nicolson schemes (scipy.linalg.solve_banded); Newton cooling, lumped and semi-infinite transients (scipy.special.erf) and Heisler’s plane-wall series; the Dittus-Boelter and Churchill-Chu correlations; Planck, Stefan-Boltzmann and Wien blackbody laws, gray-surface exchange and view factors; Neumann’s solution of the Stefan problem, Nusselt film condensation and Zuber’s critical heat flux; and LMTD and effectiveness-NTU heat exchangers.

engineeringkit.heat_transfer: conduction, convection, radiation, phase change and heat exchangers.

Fourier’s law and thermal-resistance networks, with composite walls, radial shells, critical insulation and Harper-Brown fins; finite-difference conduction (explicit, implicit, Crank-Nicolson); Newton cooling, lumped and semi-infinite transients and Heisler’s plane-wall series; Dittus-Boelter and Churchill-Chu convection correlations; blackbody laws (Planck, Stefan-Boltzmann, Wien), gray-surface exchange and view factors; Neumann’s solution of the Stefan problem, Nusselt film condensation and Zuber’s critical heat flux; and LMTD and effectiveness-NTU exchangers. Models are hand-written closed forms; SciPy supplies erf, root bracketing and the banded solver.

  • h_cold (float, optional) – Film coefficients [W/(m^2 K)], positive; None omits that film.

Returns:

Return type:

engineeringkit.heat_transfer.conduction_1d(T_initial, length, alpha, dt, steps, T_left=None, T_right=None, method='crank_nicolson')[source]#

Transient one-dimensional conduction by finite differences (explicit, implicit or Crank-Nicolson).

Solves \(\partial T/\partial t = \alpha\,\partial^2 T/\partial x^2\) on evenly spaced nodes with the \(\theta\)-scheme

\[(I - \theta r A)\,T^{n+1} = (I + (1-\theta) r A)\,T^{n}, \qquad r = \frac{\alpha\,\Delta t}{\Delta x^2},\]

where \(A\) is the second-difference matrix and \(\theta\) is 0 (explicit, stable only for \(r \le 1/2\)), 1 (fully implicit) or 1/2 (Crank-Nicolson, unconditionally stable and second order in time).

Parameters:

  • T_initial (array_like) – Initial temperature at each node [K], at least three nodes spanning 0 to length.

  • length (float) – Domain length [m], positive.

  • alpha (float) – Thermal diffusivity [m^2/s], positive.

  • dt (float) – Time step [s], positive.

  • steps (int) – Number of time steps, at least one.

  • T_left (float, optional) – Fixed surface temperature [K] at x = 0 and x = length; None (the default) makes that face adiabatic.

  • T_right (float, optional) – Fixed surface temperature [K] at x = 0 and x = length; None (the default) makes that face adiabatic.

  • method ({"crank_nicolson", "implicit", "explicit"}, optional) – Time-stepping scheme.

Returns:

Node positions, times, the temperature history, the scheme and the mesh Fourier number \(r\).

Return type:

ConductionResult

Notes

Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Heat transfer: absolute temperatures and resistance accounting. The explicit scheme is not refused above \(r = 1/2\); it is left to grow so that the instability can be seen.

References

J. Crank and P. Nicolson, “A practical method for numerical evaluation of solutions of partial differential equations of the heat-conduction type,” Proc. Cambridge Phil. Soc. 43 (1947) 50-67.

Examples

>>> result = conduction_1d([300.0] * 11, 0.1, 1e-5, 1.0, 100, T_left=400.0, T_right=400.0)
>>> round(float(result.T[-1].min()), 1)
352.6

engineeringkit.heat_transfer.neumann_stefan_front(t, alpha, Ste)[source]#

Position of the phase front in the one-phase Stefan problem (Neumann’s solution).

A liquid at its melting point fills \(x > 0\); at \(t = 0\) the face \(x = 0\) is held \(\Delta T\) below the melting point and a solid layer grows (or, the other way round, a solid at its melting point melts). The front is at

\[s(t) = 2\lambda\sqrt{\alpha t}, \qquad \lambda\,e^{\lambda^2}\operatorname{erf}\lambda = \frac{Ste}{\sqrt\pi},\]

and the temperature in the new phase is \(T_s + \Delta T\,\operatorname{erf}(x/2\sqrt{\alpha t})/\operatorname{erf}\lambda\).

Parameters:

  • t (float or array_like) – Time [s], nonnegative.

  • alpha (float) – Thermal diffusivity of the growing phase [m^2/s], positive.

  • Ste (float) – Stefan number of the growing phase [-], positive (see stefan_number()).

Returns:

Front position [m]. For small \(Ste\) this tends to Stefan’s quasi-steady \(\sqrt{2\,Ste\,\alpha t}\).

Return type:

float or ndarray

Notes

Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Heat transfer: absolute temperatures and resistance accounting. Densities of the two phases are taken equal, and the original phase does not conduct.

References

J. Stefan, “Über die Theorie der Eisbildung, insbesondere über die Eisbildung im Polarmeere,” Ann. Phys. Chem. 42 (1891) 269-286.

Examples

>>> round(float(neumann_stefan_front(86400.0, 1.2e-6, 0.1257)) * 100, 2)
15.82

Nusselt’s average coefficient for laminar film condensation on a vertical wall.

\[\bar h = 0.943\left[\frac{g\,\rho_l(\rho_l - \rho_v)\,k_l^3\,h_{fg}} {\mu_l\,L\,(T_{sat} - T_s)}\right]^{1/4}\]
