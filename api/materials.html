engineeringkit.materials#

Domain hub · History · Examples · API reference

Normal stress and strain, Hooke’s law, Lamé’s isotropic constants and Duhamel’s thermal stress; resilience, toughness and the measured stress-strain curve with its offset yield strength; the Tresca and von Mises yield criteria; the Voigt and Reuss rules of mixtures; Basquin’s S-N law, Goodman’s mean-stress line, the Coffin-Manson strain-life curve and Palmgren-Miner damage; Griffith’s fracture stress, Irwin’s stress intensity factor and Paris crack growth integrated over a crack’s life (scipy.integrate.cumulative_trapezoid); Norton creep and the Larson-Miller parameter; Frenkel’s ideal shear strength, the Peierls stress, Hall-Petch strengthening and the Ramberg-Osgood curve.

engineeringkit.materials: elasticity, yielding, fracture, fatigue, creep, plasticity and composites.

Young’s modulus, Lamé’s isotropic constants and Duhamel’s thermal stress; Tresca’s and von Mises’s yield criteria; the Voigt and Reuss bounds on a composite; Basquin’s S-N law, Goodman’s mean-stress line, the Coffin-Manson strain-life law and Palmgren-Miner damage; Griffith’s and Irwin’s fracture mechanics and Paris’s crack growth; Norton creep and the Larson-Miller parameter; Frenkel’s ideal strength, the Peierls stress, Hall-Petch strengthening and the Ramberg-Osgood curve. Models are hand-written closed forms; SciPy’s trapezoid rules integrate measured curves and crack growth.

engineeringkit.materials.crack_growth_life(C, m, delta_sigma, a0, af, Y=1.0, points=2001)[source]#

Integrate Paris’s law from an initial to a final crack length.

\[N = \int_{a_0}^{a_f} \frac{da}{C\,(Y(a)\,\Delta\sigma\sqrt{\pi a})^m}\]
Parameters:

  • C (float) – Paris coefficient [m/cycle per (Pa sqrt(m))^m], positive.

  • m (float) – Paris exponent [-], positive.

  • delta_sigma (float) – Stress range [Pa], positive.

  • a0 (float) – Initial crack length [m], positive.

  • af (float) – Final crack length [m], greater than a0; often critical_crack_length() at the peak stress.

  • Y (float | Callable) – Geometry factor [-], a positive constant or a function Y(a) of crack length [m] (for example a finite-width correction).

  • points (int) – Number of geometrically spaced crack lengths, at least 2.

Returns:

Crack length against cycles.

Return type:

CrackGrowthResult

Notes

The trapezoid rule on a geometric grid; with a constant Y and \(m \ne 2\) the closed form \(N = 2(a_0^{1-m/2} - a_f^{1-m/2}) / ((m - 2) C (Y\Delta\sigma\sqrt\pi)^m)\) checks it. Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Materials: elasticity, yield, fatigue, fracture and creep conventions.

engineeringkit.materials.ramberg_osgood_strain(stress, E, yield_strength, n, offset=0.002)[source]#

Total strain on a Ramberg-Osgood stress-strain curve.

\[\varepsilon = \frac{\sigma}{E} + \varepsilon_o\left(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_y}\right)^n\]
Parameters:

  • stress (float or array_like) – Stress [Pa]; the curve is odd, so compression mirrors tension.

  • E (float or array_like) – Young’s modulus [Pa], positive.

  • yield_strength (float or array_like) – Offset yield strength \(\sigma_y\) [Pa], positive.

  • n (float or array_like) – Hardening exponent [-], at least 1 (large n is nearly elastic-perfectly plastic).

  • offset (float or array_like) – Plastic strain \(\varepsilon_o\) at \(\sigma_y\) [-], positive; 0.002 is the 0.2% proof stress.

Returns:

Strain [-], elastic plus plastic.

Return type:

float or ndarray

Notes

Ramberg and Osgood wrote \(\varepsilon = \sigma/E + K(\sigma/E)^n\); the offset form is the same curve with \(K = \varepsilon_o (E/\sigma_y)^n\). Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Materials: elasticity, yield, fatigue, fracture and creep conventions.

References

W. Ramberg and W. R. Osgood, “Description of stress-strain curves by three parameters,” NACA Technical Note 902 (1943).

Examples
