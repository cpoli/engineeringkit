engineeringkit.signal_processing.bilinear_transform(b, a, fs, prewarp=None)[source]#

Digital filter \(H(z)\) from an analog \(H(s)\) by the bilinear transform, written out.

Parameters:

  • b (array_like) – Analog numerator and denominator coefficients in descending powers of \(s\) [-], with a of degree at least that of b.

  • a (array_like) – Analog numerator and denominator coefficients in descending powers of \(s\) [-], with a of degree at least that of b.

  • fs (float) – Sampling rate [Hz], positive.

  • prewarp (float, optional) – Frequency [Hz], below \(f_s/2\), at which the digital response is made to equal the analog one exactly. Without it \(K = 2f_s\).

Returns:

(b, a): coefficients in \(z^{-1}\) [-], with a[0] = 1.

Return type:

tuple of ndarray

Notes

Substituting \(s = K(1 - z^{-1})/(1 + z^{-1})\) and multiplying through by \((1 + z^{-1})^N\), each term \(c_m s^m\) of degree \(N\) polynomials becomes \(c_m K^m (1 - z^{-1})^m (1 + z^{-1})^{N-m}\). The left half \(s\)-plane maps inside the unit circle, so stable analog filters give stable digital ones, and the \(j\Omega\) axis maps onto the circle with \(\Omega = K\tan(\omega/2)\). Prewarping at \(f_0\) sets \(K = 2\pi f_0/\tan(\pi f_0/f_s)\). The result equals scipy.signal.bilinear with fs = K/2. See prewarp_frequency() and Signal processing: sampled energy, spectra and quantization.

References

R. M. Golden and J. F. Kaiser, “Design of wideband sampled-data filters,” Bell Syst. Tech. J. 43 (1964) 1533-1546.

Examples

>>> b, a = bilinear_transform([1.0], [1.0, 1.0], 0.5)
>>> b.tolist(), a.tolist()
([0.5, 0.5], [1.0, 0.0])

engineeringkit.signal_processing.chebyshev1_lowpass(order, ripple_db, cutoff, fs, output='ba')[source]#

Digital Chebyshev type I low-pass filter (equiripple passband) by the bilinear transform.

Parameters:

  • order (int) – Filter order \(n\), at least 1.

  • ripple_db (float) – Peak-to-peak passband ripple \(R\) [dB], positive.

  • cutoff (float) – Passband edge [Hz], where the gain last equals \(-R\) dB, between 0 and \(f_s/2\).

  • fs (float) – Sampling rate [Hz], positive.

  • output ({"ba", "sos"}, optional) – Polynomials or second-order sections, as in butterworth_lowpass().

Returns:

(b, a) or second-order sections [-].

Return type:

tuple of ndarray or ndarray

Notes

The analog prototype has \(|H|^2 = 1/(1 + \varepsilon^2 T_n^2(\Omega/\Omega_p))\), with \(T_n\) the Chebyshev polynomial and \(\varepsilon^2 = 10^{R/10} - 1\). \(|T_n| \le 1\) on \([-1, 1]\), so the passband gain swings \(n\) times between 1 and \(1/\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2}\); outside it \(T_n\) grows as fast as any polynomial of degree \(n\) can, which is why the transition is steeper than Butterworth’s for the same order. Frequencies are mapped by the bilinear transform with the edge prewarped. See Signal processing: sampled energy, spectra and quantization.

References

P. L. Chebyshev, “Théorie des mécanismes connus sous le nom de parallélogrammes,” Mém. Acad. Sci. St.-Pétersbourg, Savants étrangers 7 (1854) 539-568; A. V. Oppenheim and R. W. Schafer, Discrete-Time Signal Processing, 3rd ed., Pearson (2010), sec. 7.3.

Examples

>>> b, a = chebyshev1_lowpass(4, 1.0, 100.0, 1000.0)
>>> f, H = frequency_response(b, a, worN=[0.0, 100.0], fs=1000.0)
>>> np.round(20 * np.log10(np.abs(H)), 6).tolist()

engineeringkit.signal_processing.dct(x)[source]#

Orthonormal DCT-II \(X_k = \alpha_k \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_n \cos\frac{\pi(2n+1)k}{2N}\), written out.

Parameters:

x (array_like) – Real 1D samples [signal units].

Returns:

Coefficients \(X_0..X_{N-1}\) [signal units], with \(\alpha_0 = \sqrt{1/N}\) and \(\alpha_k = \sqrt{2/N}\).

Return type:

ndarray

Notes

The basis is orthonormal, so \(\sum X_k^2 = \sum x_n^2\) and idct() is the transpose. The DCT is the DFT of the record extended by its mirror image, which has no jump at the ends; the DFT’s implicit periodic extension does, so its coefficients fall off far more slowly for a smooth signal. For a first-order Markov process with correlation \(\rho \to 1\) the DCT tends to the Karhunen-Loève transform. Evaluated as an \(N \times N\) matrix product, so \(O(N^2)\); equal to scipy.fft.dct(x, norm="ortho"). See Signal processing: sampled energy, spectra and quantization.

References

N. Ahmed, T. Natarajan and K. R. Rao, “Discrete cosine transform,” IEEE Trans. Comput. C-23 (1974) 90-93, doi:10.1109/T-C.1974.223784.
