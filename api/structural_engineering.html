engineeringkit.structural_engineering#

Domain hub · History · Examples · API reference

Hooke’s law, Poisson’s ratio and Coulomb’s torsion for axial and twisted members; rectangular section properties, elastic and plastic; Navier’s bending stress and the simply supported beam, Clapeyron’s three-moment equation and influence lines; Euler’s buckling load and Engesser’s tangent-modulus load (scipy.optimize.brentq); plane stress transformation and Mohr’s circle; Castigliano’s theorem (scipy.integrate.simpson) and Maxwell-Betti reciprocity; Maxwell’s count, the method of joints and the direct stiffness truss with conditioning diagnostics; plastic collapse loads; Hardy Cross’s moment distribution and the plane-frame stiffness method.

engineeringkit.structural_engineering: members, beams, columns, trusses, energy methods, plasticity and frames.

Hooke’s law and Poisson’s ratio for axial members and Coulomb’s torsion; section properties; Navier’s bending theory, Clapeyron’s three-moment equation and Winkler’s influence lines; Euler’s and Engesser’s column loads; Mohr’s circle; Castigliano’s theorem and Maxwell-Betti reciprocity; Maxwell’s count, Whipple’s method of joints and the direct stiffness method for trusses; plastic collapse loads; Hardy Cross’s moment distribution and plane frames. Models are hand-written; NumPy’s linear algebra and SciPy’s Simpson rule and Brent root finder are used where they are the standard tool.

engineeringkit.structural_engineering.castigliano_deflection(dU_dP=None, *, x=None, moment=None, dmoment_dP=None, E=None, I=None)[source]#

Deflection by Castigliano’s second theorem.

Called with a single number, it returns that strain-energy derivative \(\partial U/\partial P\) unchanged (the original interface). Called with the keyword arguments, it integrates

\[\delta = \int \frac{M(x)}{E I(x)}\,\frac{\partial M}{\partial P}(x)\,dx\]

along a beam with Simpson’s rule, where \(M\) is the bending moment under the actual loads and \(\partial M/\partial P\) the moment produced by a unit load at the point and in the direction of the wanted deflection (a dummy load if no real load acts there).

Parameters:

  • dU_dP (float, optional) – Strain-energy derivative [m], returned unchanged.

  • x (array_like, optional) – Stations along the beam [m], strictly increasing. An odd number of equally spaced stations, with a station at every load point, integrates piecewise-cubic integrands exactly.

  • moment (array_like, optional) – Bending moment at each station [N m].

  • dmoment_dP (array_like, optional) – Derivative of the moment with respect to the load [m] (N m/N).

  • E (float or array_like, optional) – Young’s modulus [Pa], positive.

  • I (float or array_like, optional) – Second moment of area [m^4], positive; may vary along the beam.

Returns:

Deflection [m] in the direction of the load P.

Return type:

float

Notes

Assumptions, governing equations and validity limits are discussed in Structural engineering: loads, signs and linear elasticity. SI inputs are required; raw numbers carry no dimensional metadata. Only bending energy is included; axial and shear energy are negligible for slender beams.

References

A. Castigliano, Théorie de l’équilibre des systèmes élastiques et ses applications (Turin: Negro, 1879).

engineeringkit.structural_engineering.method_of_joints(nodes, members, loads, fixed_dofs)[source]#

Member forces of a statically determinate plane truss, joint by joint.

This is the hand method. When the truss has three reactions, they are first found from the equilibrium of the whole truss. Then, repeatedly, a joint with at most two unknown forces is chosen and its two equilibrium equations, \(\sum F_x = 0\) and \(\sum F_y = 0\), are solved for them, until every joint has been used. No stiffness enters: the forces of a determinate truss follow from statics alone.

Parameters:

  • nodes (array_like, shape (n, 2)) – Joint coordinates [m].

  • members (sequence of (int, int)) – Zero-based joint indices of each member.

  • loads (array_like) – Applied joint loads [N], 2n values or shape (n, 2).

  • fixed_dofs (array_like of int) – Restrained degrees of freedom; each carries one reaction.

Returns:

Member forces [N] (tension positive), reactions [N] at every degree of freedom (zero where free) and the order in which joints were used.

Return type:

JointMethodResult

Raises:

ValueError – If Maxwell’s count is not zero, or if the procedure stalls because every remaining joint has three or more unknowns, or two unknown members that are collinear (use PlanarTruss, or the method of sections, for such trusses).

Notes

Assumptions, governing equations and validity limits are discussed in Structural engineering: loads, signs and linear elasticity. SI inputs are required; raw numbers carry no dimensional metadata.

References

  1. Whipple, A Work on Bridge Building (Utica, N.Y., 1847).

engineeringkit.structural_engineering.moment_distribution(members, fixed_joints=(), *, tol=1e-10, max_iterations=10000)[source]#

Member-end moments of a continuous beam or braced frame by Hardy Cross’s method.

Every joint is assumed restrained against translation (no sway). A joint listed in fixed_joints is also restrained against rotation; every other joint rotates freely, so a joint with a single member is a pinned end.

The fixed-end moments of a member carrying a uniform load \(w\) are \(\mp wL^2/12\). Each cycle computes the unbalanced moment at every free joint, distributes its negative to the member ends in proportion to \(4EI/L\), and carries half of each distributed moment to the far end. Cycles stop when the largest unbalance falls below tol times the largest fixed-end moment.

Parameters:

  • members (sequence of (int, int, float, float, float)) – (i, j, length, EI, w) for each member: joint indices, length [m], flexural rigidity [N m^2] and uniform transverse load [N/m]. For a horizontal member drawn from i on the left to j on the right, positive w acts downward.

  • fixed_joints (sequence of int, optional) – Joints restrained against rotation (built-in supports).

  • tol (float, optional) – Relative convergence tolerance [-].

  • max_iterations (int, optional) – Maximum number of cycles.

Returns:

End moments [N m], clockwise positive on the member end, with the number of cycles and the unbalance history.

Return type:

MomentDistributionResult

Raises:

RuntimeError – If the unbalance has not converged after max_iterations cycles.

Notes

Assumptions, governing equations and validity limits are discussed in Structural engineering: loads, signs and linear elasticity. SI inputs are required; raw numbers carry no dimensional metadata.

References

H. Cross, “Analysis of continuous frames by distributing fixed-end moments,” Proc. ASCE 56 (1930) 919-928.

engineeringkit.structural_engineering.plastic_collapse_load(Mp, L, supports='fixed', load='point', position=None)[source]#

Collapse load of a prismatic beam with plastic moment \(M_p\).

  • "simple": \(P_c = M_pL/(ab)\) for a point load at \(a\) (\(b = L - a\)), \(w_c = 8M_p/L^2\) for a uniform load.

  • "propped": \(P_c = M_p(2/a + 1/b)\), \(w_c = 2(3 + 2\sqrt{2})M_p/L^2 \approx 11.66M_p/L^2\).

  • "fixed": \(P_c = 2M_pL/(ab)\), \(w_c = 16M_p/L^2\).

A propped cantilever is fixed at the left end (\(x = 0\)) and simply supported at the right.

Parameters:

  • Mp (float or array_like) – Plastic moment of the section [N m], positive (\(\sigma_y Z_p\)).

  • L (float or array_like) – Span [m], positive.

  • supports ({"fixed", "propped", "simple"}, optional) – End conditions.

  • load ({"point", "uniform"}, optional) – A single point load or a uniform load over the whole span.

  • position (float, optional) – Distance of the point load from the left end [m], strictly inside the span; midspan by default.

Returns:

Collapse load [N] for a point load, or collapse intensity [N/m] for a uniform load.

Return type:

float or ndarray

Notes

Assumptions, governing equations and validity limits are discussed in Structural engineering: loads, signs and linear elasticity. SI inputs are required; raw numbers carry no dimensional metadata. Rigid-plastic behaviour is assumed: no strain hardening, no instability and no reduction of \(M_p\) by shear or axial force.

References

G. Kazinczy, “Kísérletek befalazott tartókkal” (Experiments with clamped girders), Betonszemle 2 (1914), nos. 4-6; J. F. Baker, M. R. Horne and J. Heyman, The Steel Skeleton, vol. 2 (Cambridge, 1956).

engineeringkit.structural_engineering.tangent_modulus_load(Et, I, L, K=1.0, *, area=None)[source]#

Engesser’s tangent-modulus buckling load, \(P_t = \pi^2E_tI/(KL)^2\).

If Et is a number, the formula is evaluated directly. If it is a function of stress, the buckling stress must satisfy

\[\sigma = \frac{\pi^2E_t(\sigma)}{(KL/r)^2},\qquad r^2 = I/A,\]

which is solved for \(\sigma\) by Brent’s method between zero and the Euler stress computed with the initial modulus \(E_t(0)\).

Parameters:

  • Et (float, array_like or callable) – Tangent modulus [Pa], positive, or a function returning the tangent modulus [Pa] at a compressive stress [Pa]. A function must be positive at zero stress, nonnegative above it, and must not increase with stress.

  • I (float or array_like) – Least second moment of area [m^4], positive.

  • L (float or array_like) – Column length [m], positive.

  • K (float or array_like, optional) – Effective-length factor [-], positive.

  • area (float, optional) – Cross-sectional area [m^2], positive. Required when Et is a function; I, L and K must then be scalars.

Returns:

Critical load [N]. It equals euler_buckling_load() wherever the column buckles while still elastic.

Return type:

float or ndarray

Notes

engineeringkit.structural_engineering.three_moment_equation(spans, w=0.0, I=1.0)[source]#

Support moments of a continuous beam by Clapeyron’s three-moment equation.

For the supports \(i-1, i, i+1\) around spans \(L_i\) and \(L_{i+1}\) carrying uniform loads \(w_i, w_{i+1}\),

\[M_{i-1}\frac{L_i}{I_i} + 2M_i\left(\frac{L_i}{I_i} + \frac{L_{i+1}}{I_{i+1}}\right) + M_{i+1}\frac{L_{i+1}}{I_{i+1}} = -\frac{w_iL_i^3}{4I_i} - \frac{w_{i+1}L_{i+1}^3}{4I_{i+1}},\]

one equation per interior support, with \(M = 0\) at the two simply supported ends. The modulus is taken to be the same in every span.

Parameters:

  • spans (array_like) – Span lengths [m], positive, left to right.

  • w (float or array_like, optional) – Uniform load on each span [N/m], downward positive.

  • I (float or array_like, optional) – Second moment of area of each span [m^4], positive. Only ratios between spans matter.

Returns:

Bending moment at each of the len(spans) + 1 supports [N m], sagging positive (interior supports of a loaded beam hog, so they come out negative).

Return type:

ndarray

Notes

Assumptions, governing equations and validity limits are discussed in Structural engineering: loads, signs and linear elasticity. SI inputs are required; raw numbers carry no dimensional metadata.

References

É. Clapeyron, “Calcul d’une poutre élastique reposant librement sur des appuis inégalement espacés,” C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 45 (1857) 1076-1080.

Examples

Two equal spans: the middle support moment is \(-wL^2/8\).

>>> three_moment_equation([4.0, 4.0], w=1000.0)
array([    0., -2000.,     0.])
