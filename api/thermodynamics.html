engineeringkit.thermodynamics#

Domain hub · History · Examples · API reference

Ideal and van der Waals gases, the van der Waals critical point and the Joule-Thomson coefficient; Joule’s first law and Clausius’s entropy; the Carnot bound, the Otto, Diesel, Brayton and Rankine cycles and the Curzon-Ahlborn efficiency at maximum power; refrigerator and heat-pump COPs; Gouy-Stodola lost work and the exergy of heat; vapour pressure by Clausius-Clapeyron and Antoine; water and steam properties from IAPWS-IF97 regions 1, 2 and 4 (inverted for entropy and enthalpy with scipy.optimize.brentq); and Carrier’s psychrometrics.

engineeringkit.thermodynamics: gases, the two laws, cycles, refrigeration, exergy, steam and moist air.

Ideal and van der Waals gases with the Joule-Thomson coefficient; Joule’s first law and Clausius’s entropy; Carnot, Otto, Diesel, Brayton and Rankine cycles and the Curzon-Ahlborn efficiency at maximum power; refrigerator and heat-pump COPs; Gouy-Stodola lost work and the exergy of heat; vapour pressure by Clausius-Clapeyron and Antoine; water and steam properties from IAPWS-IF97 (regions 1 to 4); and Carrier’s psychrometrics. Models are hand-written closed forms; SciPy’s brentq inverts IF97 for entropy and enthalpy.

engineeringkit.thermodynamics.antoine_vapor_pressure(T, A, B, C)[source]#

Antoine’s vapour-pressure equation, \(\log_{10} p = A - B/(C + T)\).

Parameters:

  • T (float or array_like) – Absolute temperature [K], with \(C + T > 0\).

  • A (float) – Constant [-] for p in Pa.

  • B (float) – Constant [K].

  • C (float) – Constant [K] for T in kelvin.

Returns:

Vapour pressure [Pa].

Return type:

float or ndarray

Notes

Tabulated constants are often for mmHg and °C. To convert, add \(\log_{10} 133.322 = 2.12490\) to \(A\) and subtract 273.15 from \(C\); \(B\) is unchanged. Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Thermodynamics: total versus specific quantities and cycle states.

References

C. Antoine, “Tensions des vapeurs; nouvelle relation entre les tensions et les températures,” C. R. Acad. Sci. 107 (1888) 681-684, 778-780, 836-837.

Examples

>>> round(float(antoine_vapor_pressure(373.15, 8.07131 + 2.12490, 1730.63, 233.426 - 273.15)), -1)

engineeringkit.thermodynamics.clausius_clapeyron_pressure(T, T_ref, p_ref, h_fg, R_gas)[source]#

Vapour pressure from the integrated Clausius-Clapeyron relation.

\[p = p_{ref}\exp\left[-\frac{h_{fg}}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T} - \frac{1}{T_{ref}}\right)\right]\]
Parameters:

  • T (float or array_like) – Absolute temperature [K], positive.

  • T_ref (float) – Absolute temperature of a known saturation state [K], positive.

  • p_ref (float) – Saturation pressure at T_ref [Pa], positive.

  • h_fg (float) – Latent heat of vaporization [J/kg], positive, taken as constant.

  • R_gas (float) – Specific gas constant of the vapour [J/(kg K)], positive (461.5 for water).

Returns:

Saturation pressure [Pa].

Return type:

float or ndarray

Notes

Assumes the vapour is an ideal gas, the liquid volume is negligible and \(h_{fg}\) does not vary with temperature. Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Thermodynamics: total versus specific quantities and cycle states.

References

É. Clapeyron, “Mémoire sur la puissance motrice de la chaleur,” J. École Polytechnique 14 (1834) 153-190; R. Clausius, “Über die bewegende Kraft der Wärme,” Ann. Phys. 79 (1850) 368-397, 500-524.

Examples

>>> round(float(clausius_clapeyron_pressure(363.15, 373.15, 101325.0, 2.257e6, 461.5)), -2)

engineeringkit.thermodynamics.curzon_ahlborn_efficiency(T_hot, T_cold)[source]#

Efficiency of an endoreversible engine at maximum power, \(1 - \sqrt{T_c/T_h}\).

Parameters:

  • T_hot (float or array_like) – Absolute reservoir temperatures [K], with \(T_h > T_c > 0\).

  • T_cold (float or array_like) – Absolute reservoir temperatures [K], with \(T_h > T_c > 0\).

Returns:

Thermal efficiency at maximum power output [-], independent of the two heat-transfer conductances.

Return type:

float or ndarray

Notes

The engine is a Carnot cycle that exchanges heat with its reservoirs through finite conductances, \(\dot Q = K\,\Delta T\). Run reversibly it gives Carnot efficiency at zero power; run fast it gives no work at all. Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Thermodynamics: total versus specific quantities and cycle states.

References

F. L. Curzon and B. Ahlborn, “Efficiency of a Carnot engine at maximum power output,” Am. J. Phys. 43 (1975) 22-24.

Examples

>>> round(float(curzon_ahlborn_efficiency(838.15, 298.15)), 3)
0.404

engineeringkit.thermodynamics.steam_properties(p, T=None, *, x=None, s=None, h=None)[source]#

State of water or steam from pressure and one other property (IAPWS-IF97).

Parameters:

  • p (float) – Pressure [Pa], positive and at most 100 MPa.

  • T (Exactly one of) – Temperature [K], 273.15 K to 1073.15 K, for a single-phase state.

  • x (float, optional) – Vapour quality [-] in [0, 1] for a saturated mixture; needs 611.213 Pa <= p <= 22.064 MPa (the critical pressure).

  • s (float, optional) – Specific entropy [J/(kg K)], e.g. after an isentropic turbine.

  • h (float, optional) – Specific enthalpy [J/kg], e.g. after a throttling valve.

  • T

  • x

  • given. (s and h is)

Returns:

Pressure, temperature, specific volume, enthalpy, entropy, internal energy, isobaric heat capacity (NaN in the dome), quality (NaN outside it) and the IF97 region.

Return type:

SteamState

Notes

Given T, the phase is liquid if p is at or above the saturation pressure and vapour below it; a state exactly on the saturation line needs x. Region 3 (above 350 °C and above the B23 boundary pressure) is a Helmholtz function of density and temperature, so its density is found by Brent’s method on the isotherm, taking the liquid or vapour root of its van der Waals loop below the critical temperature. Given s or h, the temperature is found by Brent’s method, since both rise monotonically with temperature at fixed pressure. Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Thermodynamics: total versus specific quantities and cycle states.

References

W. Wagner et al., “The IAPWS Industrial Formulation 1997 for the thermodynamic properties of water and steam,” J. Eng. Gas Turbines Power 122 (2000) 150-182.

Examples

>>> round(st.h / 1e3, 1), round(st.s / 1e3, 4), st.region

Van der Waals pressure \(p = nRT/(V - nb) - an^2/V^2\).

Utilities#

IAPWS-IF97 basic equations for regions 1 to 4, in reduced variables.

The coefficients are those of the IAPWS release (Lucerne 2007 revision of the 1997 formulation): region 1 is compressed liquid, region 2 superheated vapour, region 3 the near-critical fluid and region 4 the saturation line. Regions 1 and 2 are dimensionless Gibbs free energies \(\gamma(\pi, \tau)\) and region 3 a dimensionless Helmholtz free energy \(\phi(\delta, \tau)\); every property follows from the function and its derivatives. Pressures are in MPa and temperatures in K inside this module, as in the release; the public wrappers in engineeringkit.thermodynamics.systems.steam convert to Pa and J.

Density at which the region 3 pressure equals p_mpa on the isotherm T.

Below the critical temperature an isotherm of the Helmholtz function has a van der Waals loop, so up to three densities share a pressure; the largest is the liquid and the smallest the vapour.

Density [kg/m^3].

Return type:

float

Notes
