engineeringkit.thermodynamics: gases, the two laws, cycles, refrigeration, exergy, steam, moist air and combustion.

engineeringkit.thermodynamics.adiabatic_flame_temperature(c, h, o=0.0, *, h_f_fuel, excess_air=0.0, T_air=298.15)[source]#

Temperature of the products of complete, adiabatic, constant-pressure combustion.

Parameters:

  • c (float) – Atoms of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen per fuel molecule [-].

  • h (float) – Atoms of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen per fuel molecule [-].

  • o (float) – Atoms of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen per fuel molecule [-].

  • h_f_fuel (float) – Standard enthalpy of formation of the fuel at 298.15 K [J/mol].

  • excess_air (float, optional) – Excess air as a fraction of the stoichiometric amount [-], nonnegative.

  • T_air (float, optional) – Temperature of the combustion air [K], 298.15 K to 1500 K; the fuel enters at 298.15 K.

Returns:

Adiabatic flame temperature [K].

Return type:

float

Notes

Solves \(\sum_p n_i[h_{f,i} + \Delta h_i(T)] = h_f^{fuel} + \sum_r n_j\Delta h_j(T_{air})\) by Brent’s method, with water as vapour and no dissociation. Above about 2000 K real flames dissociate CO2 and H2O, so the measured temperature is lower (methane in air: about 2230 K at equilibrium against 2327 K here). Every parameter is scalar-only: the balance is solved for one fuel and mixture. Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Thermodynamics: total versus specific quantities and cycle states.

References

Y. A. Çengel and M. A. Boles, Thermodynamics: An Engineering Approach, 9th ed., New York: McGraw-Hill (2019), Ch. 15.

Examples

>>> round(adiabatic_flame_temperature(1, 4, h_f_fuel=-74.87e3))
2327

engineeringkit.thermodynamics.brayton_cycle(pressure_ratio, T_min, T_max, *, compressor_efficiency=1.0, turbine_efficiency=1.0, regenerator_effectiveness=0.0, compressor_stages=1, turbine_stages=1, cp=1005.0, gamma=1.4, p_min=101325.0)[source]#

Air-standard gas-turbine cycle with regeneration, intercooling and reheat.

Parameters:

  • pressure_ratio (float) – Overall compressor pressure ratio [-], greater than one.

  • T_min (float) – Compressor inlet temperature [K], positive; every intercooler returns the air to it.

  • T_max (float) – Turbine inlet temperature [K], above the compressor outlet temperature; every reheater returns the gas to it.

  • compressor_efficiency (float, optional) – Isentropic efficiency of each stage [-] in (0, 1].

  • turbine_efficiency (float, optional) – Isentropic efficiency of each stage [-] in (0, 1].

  • regenerator_effectiveness (float, optional) – Regenerator effectiveness [-] in [0, 1]; zero for no regenerator. It heats the compressed air by \(\varepsilon(T_{exhaust} - T_{compressor})\) when the exhaust is the hotter stream and does nothing otherwise.

  • compressor_stages (int, optional) – Number of compressor stages (with an intercooler between each pair) and turbine stages (with a reheater between each pair), each with the same pressure ratio \(r_p^{1/N}\).

  • turbine_stages (int, optional) – Number of compressor stages (with an intercooler between each pair) and turbine stages (with a reheater between each pair), each with the same pressure ratio \(r_p^{1/N}\).

  • cp (float, optional) – Isobaric specific heat [J/(kg K)], positive.

  • gamma (float, optional) – Ratio of specific heats [-], greater than one.

  • p_min (float, optional) – Compressor inlet pressure [Pa], positive; it only sets the pressure labels of the states.

Returns:

State temperatures, pressures and entropies; efficiency; compressor and turbine work, heat added and heat regenerated per kilogram.

Return type:

BraytonResult

Notes

With ideal components and a perfect regenerator the efficiency is \(1 - (T_{min}/T_{max})\,r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}\), which falls with pressure ratio; with many intercooled and reheated stages it approaches the Ericsson cycle and Carnot’s bound. Pressure losses in the heat exchangers are neglected. Every parameter is scalar-only: one cycle is built with its list of states. Inputs are raw SI numbers with no dimensional metadata; see Thermodynamics: total versus specific quantities and cycle states.

References

Y. A. Çengel and M. A. Boles, Thermodynamics: An Engineering Approach, 9th ed., New York: McGraw-Hill (2019), Ch. 9.

Examples

>>> res = brayton_cycle(8.0, 300.0, 1300.0, compressor_efficiency=0.8, turbine_efficiency=0.85)
>>> round(res.efficiency, 3), round(res.back_work_ratio, 3)
(0.274, 0.615)

Utilities#

IAPWS-IF97 basic equations for regions 1 to 5, in reduced variables.

The coefficients are those of the IAPWS release (Lucerne 2007 revision of the 1997 formulation): region 1 is compressed liquid, region 2 superheated vapour, region 3 the near-critical fluid, region 4 the saturation line and region 5 the high-temperature steam of gas turbines. Regions 1, 2 and 5 are dimensionless Gibbs free energies \(\gamma(\pi, \tau)\) and region 3 a dimensionless Helmholtz free energy \(\phi(\delta, \tau)\); every property follows from the function and its derivatives. Pressures are in MPa and temperatures in K inside this module, as in the release; the public wrappers in engineeringkit.thermodynamics.systems.steam convert to Pa and J.
